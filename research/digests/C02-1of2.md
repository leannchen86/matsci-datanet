## 10. Jargon that matters
- Powder XRD — X-ray fingerprint of a powder; a signal to be explained as a mixture of known templates.
- Phase identification — which crystalline compounds are in the sample (multi-label classification without ground truth).
- Rietveld refinement / Rwp — fitting the pattern with candidate structures; Rwp = fit error (a good fit can still be wrong).
- Reference structure DB (COD, ICSD, ICDD, CSD, MP) — the template library; licences decide AI use.
- Harness — agent scaffold: tools, databases, lab context.
- Midtraining — continued pretraining on domain data before RL.
- LLM-as-judge — a model grades outputs; here both metric and reward (Goodhart risk).
- Held-out chemical system — split by element set; a group split.
- Enantiomorph — mirror-image crystal; identical powder pattern.
- Known-answer sample — mixture weighed from known ingredients; the only true ground truth.
- Round robin — many labs measure one sample; gives inter-lab noise.
- PDB + CASP — open archive plus neutral blind contest; preferred analogue over ImageNet.

## 12. Smart or non-obvious ideas worth not losing
1. The scarce asset is task-specific supervision with label provenance plus synthesis context, not patterns or structures; a public effort must build it on purpose.
2. Periodic published priors a public benchmark can reuse: 77.2% inter-expert agreement, "hours per pattern", five phases per accepted fit, ~4x from context + references on a fixed model.
3. A licence-clean reference layer (COD CC0 + MP CC BY + computed) is a concrete differentiator now that CSD/ICSD training rights are exclusive to one company.
4. CASP-size (100-300 item) expert-curated tests are normal; value comes from neutrality, hidden answers and per-item results.
5. Press-release benchmark checks any ML person can run: is the score k/n, do base rates match across charts, SE-of-difference z, judges-as-contestants, matched cost bases.
