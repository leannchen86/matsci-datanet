# R07 — Thermoelectric Benchmark Spec (draft 0.2, 2026-09-15)

## 1. What this slice is (2-3 plain sentences)
- A published report proposing the first *blind* thermoelectric (TE) benchmark: models predict Seebeck coefficient S(T) and resistivity ρ(T) for specimens whose labels are newly measured, unpublished, replicated across labs, and scored against between-lab noise. It argues by analogy to what ImageNet had (raw inputs, accepted labels, cheap label check, known noise floor, fixed task, hidden labels, open license), then tests the plan on real open data: Starrydata2 ThermoelectricMaterials (55,422 samples / 156,721 curves / 9,512 papers, snapshot 2026-09-14), teMatDb272 and ESTM (5,205 rows).
- Self-labelled status: "Verified" = preconditions map, family choice, all section-3 tests, fill rates, metrology figures, quoted precedents. "Draft/Inference" = family-layer field list, the 12 rules (R1-R12), the scoring formula. "Not done" = open issues in the shared specimen core, 17 missing rules, pilot lab, cost estimate, organizer baselines. Footer: "Companion to the Materials Record Wall. Draft for discussion; not a released standard."

## 4. Findings (each: claim — numbers/evidence — [STATUS] — so what)
- F1. The physics identity zT = S²T/(ρκ) works as a cheap automatic label check at scale — Starrydata samples with declared zT > 0.05, n = 13,702: ≤10% deviation 11,587; 10-20% 801; 20-50% 668; >50% 646. Experiment-tagged subset n = 11,004: 9,307 / 642 / 529 / 526. teMatDb272 (already Sc-ZT filtered) n = 271: 270 within 10%, 1 in 10-20%, 0 above. Of the 646 >50% cases, 266 have a clean power-of-ten ratio (unit errors). Simplified version of teMatDb's Sc-ZT protocol (which kept 10,840 of 15,532 Starrydata samples, 20250501 snapshot) — [OWN-EXPERIMENT] — TE has a built-in "checksum" that most materials properties lack; ~85% of checked samples pass, the rest are flaggable.
- F2. Figure digitization is NOT the bottleneck — teMatDb and Starrydata digitized the same figures independently; on 100 label-matched pairs (peak zT): 98 of 100 within 2%, median |diff| 0.45%, 90th percentile 0.97%, largest 15.0% (cause not found). 20 composition-only matches: largest gap 32% (different specimens from same paper) — [OWN-EXPERIMENT] — reading plots adds ~0.5% error vs 15-20% lab-to-lab; literature pools are fine for training, but a test set needs new replicated measurements [OPINION/inference].
- F3. Specimen descriptors are mostly missing — over all 55,422 Starrydata samples (presence only, not correctness): Form 22,723 (41.0%); FabricationProcess 16,117 (29.1%); ElectricalMeasurement 13,727 (24.8%; 4-probe 10,680); ThermalMeasurement 9,421 (17.0%; LaserFlash 5,143, LaserFlash+DSC 1,789); Purity 4,695 (8.5%); RelativeDensity 2,419 (4.4%, binned strings like "=95%" 878, "%" 782); GrainSize 929 (1.7%); direction 12 (0.02%, 9 of 12 are "-"); Measurement axis 1 (one value "c"). teMatDb272 has no uncertainty, method, density, direction or Cp column; ESTM has no specimen descriptors — [OWN-EXPERIMENT] — the inputs a model would need to tell two same-formula specimens apart barely exist.
- F4. Layer fill rates in three sources (Starrydata Experiment 32,435 / teMatDb272 272 / ESTM 5,205 rows): 7 of 28 layer fields have no column in ANY source (parent batch/pellet id; measured composition; step T/p/time/atmosphere as fields; density used for κ; measured-vs-calculated flag; per-property uncertainty; measuring lab). In Starrydata Experiment only 9 fields are filled on ≥ half the samples. Selected Starrydata-Experiment rates: processing route 48.5% (15,717), electrical method 40.5% (13,142), thermal method 28.2% (9,133), phase purity 14.0%, relative density any form 7.5% (2,427) / as a number 2.5% (821), Cp source stated 6.3% (2,059, heuristic), grain size 2.9%, direction <0.1% (1). Values: S 80.2% (26,021), ρ/σ 78.2% (25,367), κ 52.4% (16,998), zT 47.9% (15,541). Starrydata specimen id is unique per digitized sample per paper only (same physical specimen in another paper gets another id); ESTM has no sample key, 49 (formula, temperature) pairs repeat — [OWN-EXPERIMENT].
- F5. Record integrity problems — 2,622 curves with no finite points (787 samples entirely empty); 240 Seebeck curves outside 1e-7 to 1e-2 V/K; 770 curves with T outside 1-2000 K; 1 confirmed name-composition contradiction ("RuTe2" vs "RuSe2", paper title says RuSe2); 3 test records (e.g. "ALD_TEST_sample"); 364 samples with battery descriptors inside the TE project; ESTM: 3,853 power-factor and 3,310 ZT cells are unflagged Excel formulas. DOI overlap: teMatDb∩Starrydata 193, ESTM∩Starrydata 75, all three 7 — [OWN-EXPERIMENT] — the same curves recur across "different" open datasets (leakage risk), and calculated values pose as measured.
- F6. Random splits leak; composition does not identify the label — 300 K values, Experiment samples (S: 12,222 samples / 3,015 papers; ρ: 12,158 / 3,045). Composition-only 5-NN baseline: random split → test paper in training 93.5% of the time, S median error 25.5%, within 20% 43.4%, ρ 0.24 decades, within factor 2 56.8%. Grouped by paper → 42.2%, 31.1%, 0.36 decades, 44.8%. Grouped by chemical system → 46.6%, 28.6%, 0.37, 44.3%. On the 3,593 S samples whose composition appears in training papers: kNN 29.4% < simple same-composition median lookup 22.9%; even an oracle median with answers known misses by 15.0% (S sign agreement only 92.2%) — [OWN-EXPERIMENT] — "one composition string carries many labels"; a lookup table beats the ML baseline, and there is an irreducible ~15% floor for composition-only inputs.
- F7. Same formula, different papers disagree far more than instruments do — S: 647 compositions reported by 2+ papers, 21,856 paper pairs, median difference 32.7%, only 35.6% within 20%, 3,881 (17.8%) have opposite sign; same-paper sample pairs (5,161) median 12.9%; Bi2Te3 across 111 papers: paper medians −241 to +280 µV/K. ρ: 630 compositions, 22,548 pairs, median 0.35 decades (factor 2.2), 45.7% within factor 2, 83.1% within factor 10; same-paper pairs (5,133) median 0.14 decades, 71.6% within factor 2 — [OWN-EXPERIMENT]. Compared with one-specimen round robins (S 6% standard / 5.7-7.9% at 2σ) → most spread is between specimens sharing a formula (doping, processing, orientation), not between instruments [OPINION/inference].
- F8. Inter-lab noise floor is quantified — Alleno 2015 skutterudite round robin, 300-700 K, relative standard uncertainty: S 6%, ρ 8%, κ 11%, ZT 19%. IEA-AMT half-Heusler round robin (11 labs; ZT from 9 covering 300-773 K), 2σ: 4-point S 5.7-7.9%, ρ 6.5% at 323 K / 7.4% at 773 K, ZT 11.5-16.4%; authors expect ≥20% reproducibility SD for most materials; 2-point vs 4-point Seebeck means differ 11.3-13.6%. Martin 2021: curated round robins may agree better than the wider community. ImageNet comparison: label error ~0.3%, human top-5 error 5.1% vs GoogLeNet 6.8% on 1,500 images — [SOURCE] — a benchmark can state which model differences are resolvable; zT is ~3x noisier than S, so start with S and ρ.
- F9. No blind TE test with new measurements found; Matbench has no TE task — explicitly "a negative search result, marked unverifiable"; "none was found, not proven absent" — [SOURCE/negative search] — the slot appears open.
- F10. Computed counterpart is weak — Ricci 2017, ~48,000 materials, CC0; vs experiment Seebeck R² 0.79, constant-relaxation-time power factor R² 0.33 — [SOURCE].
- F11. Label cost — S and ρ fast (<20 s room-temperature probe; ~30 s per point at high T); κ needs diffusivity + Cp; IEA-AMT round robins took 4 months (Bi2Te3, 7 labs) to ~2 years (half-Heusler, late 2012-June 2014) — [SOURCE].
- F12. Reference materials — NIST SRM 3451 (n-type Bi2Te3) certifies S 10-390 K (e.g. −231.36 µV/K at 300.73 K, U = 6.26 µV/K, k=2); SRM 3452 (p-type SiGe) S 295-900 K, U ≈ 3% (k=2), do not measure above 900 K, 2-probe recommended because 4-probe may overestimate S; neither certifies resistivity. BCR-724 (Pyroceram 9606) certifies diffusivity (U 6.1%) and conductivity (U 6.5%, k=2) to 1025 K; values lapse after ≥10 heatings; Cp not certified — [SOURCE].
- F13. Scoring statistics — with three labs and s* = σpt, u(xpt) = 1.25·s*/√p ≈ 0.72σpt, well above the 0.3σpt threshold, so z′ (not z) is required and σpt should be frozen from earlier round robins; ISO 13528: |score| ≤2 acceptable, 2-3 warning, ≥3 action; EA-4/21: scoring on ≤7 labs' results without external reference generally not recommended — [SOURCE + OPINION].
- F14. Precedent verification pass: 285 claims confirmed, 35 corrected. Example corrections recorded: SAMPL "no anonymous submissions" dates from SAMPL6 (2017-2019), "not specifically 2018"; SAMPL9 cyclodextrin data corrected 3/31/2022 after the Feb 23, 2022 deadline (checked against saved README); CAGI7 assessors blinded to team AND methods, "stronger than the report's generic 'anonymized predictions'" — [REFUTED→corrected wording for those items; rest SOURCE].

## 5. Problems / gaps identified in the field or in the data
- G1. No agreed definition of a TE *specimen*; databases are flat tables keyed by composition + temperature — evidence F3/F4/F6/F7 — hurts anyone training or evaluating models (labels are ambiguous) — [OWN-EXPERIMENT].
- G2. No uncertainty / error-bar field in Starrydata, teMatDb272 or ESTM; no measuring-lab field; no measured-vs-calculated flag — hurts benchmark scoring and noise-aware training — [OWN-EXPERIMENT].
- G3. Measurement direction essentially unrecorded (12 of 55,422; axis on 1) though TE materials are often anisotropic; numeric density near-absent (821 of 32,435) though κ depends on it — [OWN-EXPERIMENT].
- G4. Standard random splits leak (93.5% own-paper-in-training) and overstate performance (S error 25.5% vs 42.2% paper-held-out) — hurts every published composition→property TE model claim — [OWN-EXPERIMENT].
- G5. Cross-dataset duplication (193 / 75 / 7 shared DOIs) and every Starrydata value is a published figure (figure_name on all 156,721 curves) → any early publication of test labels lands in the training pool — [OWN-EXPERIMENT].
- G6. Dirty records: unit errors (266 power-of-ten cases), empty curves (2,622), out-of-range values, test records, wrong-project samples (364 battery), hidden Excel formulas in ESTM — [OWN-EXPERIMENT].
- G7. No fixed task/metric and no blind challenge in TE; ESTM repository has no license (UCSB has no license field) — [SOURCE/negative search].
- G8. Spec itself is incomplete: shared specimen core has 37 weak gap-requirement links, 2 unsupported requirements, 27 other issues, none resolved; adversarial review covered only R1-R12 (not the layer), 22 major + 15 minor issues unworked; 17 missing rules; no cost estimate, no pilot lab; only baseline is the composition-only kNN — [OPINION/self-assessment].

## 7. Decisions made and things ruled out (with the reason)
- TE transport chosen as first family — only family with large open CC BY per-specimen curve pool + physical identity check + quantified inter-lab noise + no existing blind test. Runner-up alloy mechanical rejected for now (small pools, mixed licenses).
- Phase 1 restricted to S and ρ; κ and zT deferred — κ slow (diffusivity + Cp), ZT noise 19% vs 6%/8%.
- Literature (digitized) data OK for training, NOT for test — digitization error 0.45% is fine, but labels are public and specimens under-described.
- Composition alone never defines a specimen; splits must hold out whole papers — 3e evidence; teMatDb 19 "Nano" vs "SOA ingot" −27%, teMatDb 259 carrier concentration 6.4e19 vs 2.0e19 −32%.
- Use z′ not z; take σpt from prior round robins, frozen before scoring; score against labs that do not predict.
- One ranked submission per group per window, no leaderboard feedback (ILSVRC Baidu: ≥30 accounts, ≥200 submissions; ARC Prize leak argument). Anonymous entries not scored. Extra-data / later-cutoff / network-at-inference models go in a separate track.
- Default circulation: one bar in fixed order, first lab re-measures at end, homogeneity check on sister bars first; specimens with excessive lab disagreement leave ranking. No SRM 3452 reading above 900 K.
- Licenses: labels CC BY 4.0 after release; scoring code MIT.
- Module-level round robins (heat-flux uncertainty) deliberately not used yet.

## 10. Jargon that matters for the big picture
- Seebeck coefficient S — voltage per degree of temperature difference; sign tells n- vs p-type (so "opposite sign" = qualitatively wrong label).
- ρ / σ — electrical resistivity / conductivity (σ = 1/ρ); errors reported in decades (log10).
- κ — thermal conductivity; slow to measure (laser-flash diffusivity × density × heat capacity Cp).
- zT — figure of merit S²σT/κ; a derived label, so it acts like a checksum over four curves.
- Sc-ZT — teMatDb's self-consistency filter using that identity.
- Specimen vs composition — a specimen is one physical piece with its processing history; composition is just the formula. ML analogy: composition is the class name, specimen is the individual image.
- Round robin — same sample measured by many labs to get between-lab SD (= inter-annotator disagreement / label-noise floor).
- Standard uncertainty vs 2σ / k=2 — one-sigma vs ~95% coverage; do not compare across without converting.
- z′ score — (prediction − assigned value)/√(σpt² + u²(xpt)); error in units of lab noise including uncertainty of the reference value; ζ also includes the entrant's own stated uncertainty.
- σpt / xpt / u(xpt) — proficiency-testing terms: frozen between-lab SD / assigned (consensus) value / its uncertainty.
- SRM / BCR-724 — certified reference materials used to calibrate instruments (a "known-answer" test sample).
- Grouped split (by paper / chemical system) — hold out whole groups to prevent leakage, like splitting by patient rather than by scan.
- Digitized data — numbers read off published plot images (Starrydata's entire content).
- 4-probe / 2-probe / van der Pauw / Harman — electrical measurement geometries; choice shifts S by >10%.
- Family layer / specimen core — property-specific schema extension on top of a shared cross-domain specimen schema.

## 12. Smart or non-obvious ideas worth not losing
1. The oracle test: even a same-composition median chosen WITH the answers misses S by 15.0% — a clean, publishable demonstration that composition-only TE models have a hard ceiling; and a trivial lookup (22.9%) beats kNN (29.4%).
2. Separate the two noise sources with data already in hand: same-formula/different-paper spread (32.7%) vs same-specimen/different-lab (~6%) vs digitization (0.45%) → the missing information is specimen description, not better instruments or better plot reading.
3. Borrow proficiency-testing statistics (z′, frozen σpt, labs that do not predict) so ML scores are expressed in units of measurement noise — tells you when two models are statistically indistinguishable.
4. The zT identity as a free deposit-time validator (wwPDB-style validation report) — already finds 646 broken samples, 266 of them unit errors.
5. Blind the experimenters too (Sandia precedent) and freeze the specimen record before labels are measured; cancel a target if its label leaks (CAPRI/CASP), hash the answer key before the window closes.
