# R09 — Recursion in Materials AI (literature + dataset survey, compiled 15 Sep 2026)

## 1. What this slice is (2-3 plain sentences)
A published survey report answering one question: have the 2025-26 ML trends of tiny recursive models, looped transformers and data-efficiency results reached materials/chemistry AI? It catalogues 110 papers (68 "direct" transfers) in six threads, a source-checked catalog of 56 datasets (size, labels, licence, access), and a list of searches that came back empty. It contains NO experiments by the sessions: every number is "as reported by each paper", not reproduced.

## 2. What the user asked / wanted (bullets; quote short phrases of their own words)
- No user dialogue in the report. The framing question (subtitle): "Is anything like tiny recursive models, looped transformers or the new data-efficiency results happening in materials and chemistry AI? What exists, how close each paper is to the ML trend, and what data each one used."

## 4. Findings (each: claim — numbers/evidence — [STATUS] — "so what")
Thread sizes (papers / direct): Looped & weight-tied 14/9; Fixed points 18/2; Test-time compute 18/11; Reasoning & RL 15/14; Data efficiency & scaling 25/18; Small-data loops 20/14. Total 110/68. [SOURCE]

- Headline: "Yes, but the direct transfers are few and small." Looped/TRM-style materials models (TRIADS, SMolLM, SR-CGCNN, Lim FEA surrogates, SeqBattNet, SCORE, iterated-map fine-tuning): none clearly beats the best conventional model; the pitch is similar accuracy with far fewer parameters. No TRM/HRM work on crystal structure prediction, interatomic potentials or retrosynthesis. [OPINION on SOURCE] — a nearly empty niche, but weak evidence that looping helps.
- SMolLM: 53K-param shared block applied 8x gets 95.3% valid SMILES on ZINC-250K vs 87.6% for a 527K unshared GPT; the 3.2M unshared GPT still leads (99.4%). Validity by depth 33/91/97/99/99% at D=1/2/4/8/16. [SOURCE] — best "looping = parameter efficiency" evidence.
- TRIADS (44K-247K params; single-author Zenodo preprint, AI-assisted, not peer-reviewed): Matbench steels MAE 91.20 ± 12.23 MPa vs MODNet 87.76, CrabNet 107.32; deep supervision takes steels MAE 134.59 -> 103.29. [SOURCE]
- Negative/neutral looped results: SR-CGCNN cuts conv params 66,432 -> 22,912 but is slightly worse everywhere (MP formation energy 0.0986 vs 0.0945 eV/atom; 5 steps worse than 3; single runs, 10k subsets). Lim: TRM variants never Pareto-optimal; data/code unreleased. SeqBattNet: plain feed-forward edges it on NASA (0.024 vs 0.025-0.026). SCORE: ESOL RMSE 0.542 vs best stacked model 0.533. [SOURCE] — looping is not a free win.
- Fixed points are the long-standing materials analogue because the physics is self-consistent (SCF, charge equilibration). Only two are direct: DEQuify (OC20-200k force MAE 0.035 vs 0.038 eV/A but energy MAE worse, 0.498 vs 0.392 eV; MD17 ~19% faster) and I-MLFF (force MAE 0.37/0.39 vs explicit PaiNN 0.75/0.84 kcal/mol/A on MD17/MD22 at one layer call; 2-5x less compute). [SOURCE]
- Test-time compute pays in structure prediction: Clari blind-test-5 success 0.681 -> 0.789 -> 0.975 at 30/150/400 samples; Atomistic LM MP-20 Match@1 45.6% -> best-of-20 83.2%; AtomMOF steering cuts formation-energy error 0.127 -> 0.020 eV/atom (validity 81.7% -> 91.7%); FRIGID Top-1 25.03% vs 8.34%, log-linear in inference compute; test-time adaptation cuts ISO17 MAE 40% / 31% (Cui et al.). [SOURCE] — "sample more + verify" is the most mature transfer.
- RL with verifiers is established: ether0 70% reaction prediction after 46,000 examples vs 64.1% for a model on ~480k (caveat: ORD vs USPTO, cross-dataset); Park & Walsh (Chemeleon2) mSUN 15.9% -> 61.3% on Alex-MP-20 vs MatterGen 41.0%, collapses without a diversity reward; PLaID++ S.U.N. 7.17% -> 22.27%, VASP confirms ~19.1% of 1,000; Mat-Pref SFT+GRPO 65.2/71.6/60.0% vs SFT-only 44.9/47.9/50.2%; MatSciBench DeepSeek-R1 75.22%, GPT-5 75.02%, self-correction hurt 4 of 5 non-thinking models; AtomWorld <12% on rotate_around. [SOURCE]
- Data efficiency is the largest thread: synthetic pretraining lets 25 DFT structures match 800 ("at least 32x"); CHGNet PBE->r2SCAN transfer with 1K structures beats >10K from scratch (refitting reference energies lifts Pearson 0.09 -> 0.93); frozen MACE-MP fine-tune on 664 configs matches scratch on 3,376; MatterSim 30 water configs match 900; NequIP water from 133 structures vs DeepMD's 133,500; distilled students >10x (GNN) / >100x (ACE) faster; data-scaling exponents rise with built-in symmetry 0.31/0.39/0.50/0.75; UMA loss log-linear over 1e18-1e20 FLOPs; "broken" scaling law on >200,000 dielectric functions. [SOURCE] — the proven recipe is "fine-tune a foundation potential on tens-hundreds of labels".
- Small-data: TabPFN (no training, Magpie descriptors) beats composition-only SOTA on 5 of 6 Matbench tasks (phonons 41.95 vs 46.58 cm-1; worse on perovskites); best/tied on 50 of 58 Polaris+MoleculeACE tasks; as active-learning surrogate wins 7 of 10 datasets per table (text says 8), mean savings 52% vs GP, 29.77% vs RF. [SOURCE]
- Agent research loops already run on Matbench: Zhang & Yakobson — Claude Code (Opus 4.8) edits one training script, 1,500 s jobs on one RTX 2070 SUPER, keeps a change only if held-out MAE improves -> mp_gap MAE 0.1480 ± 0.003 eV, best of 17 models without external pretraining, 6th overall, "rediscover known ideas". Ning et al. — 701 attempts over 10 endpoints, holdout confirmed 9 of 10; MAE -17.4% expt_gap, -18.6% steels. MLIPilot 3.2x (QM7) / 11.2x (Cu EMT); Lang2MLIP grew 60 -> 7,526 structures in 21 decisions. [SOURCE] — the most copy-able recipe for a solo ML person.
- A-Lab: after the January 2026 Author Correction, 36 of 57 targets made (63%), down from 41 of 58; materials were "new to the platform, not necessarily new to science". [SOURCE; earlier 41/58 = REFUTED] — feeds the user's autonomous-lab / data-quality thread.
- ML-side corrections: HRM's ARC-AGI-1 40.3% re-ran at 32% (ARC-AGI-2 5.0% -> 2%) [REFUTED -> 32%]; ARC Prize ablation: the outer refinement loop, not the architecture, drives gains (same-size transformer within ~5 points; 1 -> 8 loops doubles accuracy); TRM reaches most accuracy at the first recursion step and gives zero with blank puzzle IDs; HRM-Text is press-release only; Ouro: looping improves manipulation, not storage. [SOURCE]
- LM data-constrained results not yet tested in materials: ~4 epochs of repeats about as good as unique data (Muennighoff); 2.29x data efficiency, 5.17x with ensembles (Kim); diffusion tolerates far more repetition, R_D* 512.85 vs 31.93 (Prabhudesai); scarce data reusable ~15-20 times (Sedova). [SOURCE]

## 5. Problems / gaps identified in the field or in the data
- Ten searches "found nothing" [SOURCE = survey result; absence only as good as the search]:
  1. TRM/HRM/GRAM/PTRM/TARM on crystals, molecules, CSP, retrosynthesis or potentials (OpenAlex: 5 TRM citers, 3 HRM citers, none in materials; of HRM's 144 Semantic Scholar citers only SeqBattNet qualified; of Huginn's 361 only SMolLM and LatentChem).
  2. Looped depth with test-time scaling in atomistic models.
  3. Adaptive computation time / early exit in GNN potentials.
  4. Universal-Transformer/ALBERT-style sharing beyond PSMolBERT, SMolLM, UTGN.
  5. Coconut-style latent reasoning beyond LatentChem.
  6. DEQ potentials other than DEQuify and I-MLFF.
  7. Learned SCF mixing / neural Anderson acceleration.
  8. A Muennighoff-style repeated-data study for interatomic potentials (closest: Xu et al., molecular LMs).
  9. Diffusion-vs-autoregressive data-efficiency comparison on materials.
  10. Curves of S.U.N. rate vs sampling budget, beyond a few papers.
- Looped-model evidence is thin: small models, small benchmarks, mostly preprints, several single-run or no-code — hurts anyone building on them. [OPINION on SOURCE]
- Cross-paper comparison unsafe: "Baselines, splits and budgets differ, so compare within a paper, not across papers." [SOURCE]
- Data is mostly public DFT sets (MP-20, MPtrj, OMat24, OMol25, QM9, MD17/MD22, Matbench) plus small bespoke sets "often unreleased". Not released: ether0's 640,730 RL problems (only a 325-row benchmark), MatterSim's ~17M points, Stress10K, Warpage10K. Licence-walled: CSD (>1.4M). Gated: OMol25, ODAC25, ChemCoT. Non-commercial: GNoME. [SOURCE] — hurts reproducibility and reuse.
- Reader's count (not stated in the report): only ~10 of 56 datasets carry experimental labels (4 of 13 Matbench tasks — steels 312, expt_gap 4,604, expt_is_metal 4,921, glass 5,680 — CSD, CCDC blind tests, ESOL 1,128, three battery sets, USPTO-50K, ether0 sources); none is XRD patterns or thermoelectrics. [OPINION]
- Headline dataset sizes conflict even at source: OMat24 "100.8M" is train only (~118M total; abstract >110M); OMol25 says both >140M and >100M (train 140,641,161; UMA used 75,889,983); ODAC25 v2 ~60M vs v1/docs ~70M; Alex-MP-20 607,683 vs 607,684; MPtrj blurb "1.3M" vs record 1,580,395; WBM 257,487 -> 256,963 -> 215,488 unique prototypes; ChemCoT and C-SYNTH-23M licences differ across hosts. [SOURCE]
- Label/benchmark hygiene: rMD17 README says results trained on 50k MD17 samples "should be considered meaningless" and warns against >1,000 training samples; QM9 has 3,054 of 133,885 failing a geometry check; QM7-X ~4.6% duplicates; Mat-Pref notes PBE band gaps have larger systematic error than formation energies; OC20 and OC22 labels not interchangeable. [SOURCE]

## 7. Decisions made and things ruled out (with the reason)
- Scope: "recursive intelligence" = recursive reasoning models + looped/recurrent-depth transformers + recursive LMs; recursive self-improvement only via agent loops; "Ricursive Intelligence" (chip start-up, $35M seed + $300M Series A at $4B) ruled out — "Chips, not materials".
- Labels: Direct = the ML technique itself on materials/chemistry data; Analogous = same computational shape reached another way (physical fixed points, incidental weight tying, iterative refiners); Adjacent = no materials data. "Judgement calls."
- Fixed-point thread: only models iterating hidden features count as direct; those iterating physical quantities are analogous.
- 14 papers read and excluded as out of scope (e.g. RecCrysFormer, UCell, FastCSP, MolCrystalFlow, TFMat); 6 borderline not listed (ASTRA, ReMDM, uFlowCSP, PhysVEC, arXiv 2501.19179, Packora).
- Numbers reported, not reproduced; Trikha et al. flagged "weigh lightly".

## 10. Jargon that matters for the big picture (<=15)
- TRM / HRM — tiny nets (5-27M params) that re-apply one block to refine an answer; an RNN over "thinking steps".
- Looped / weight-tied / recurrent depth — one layer reused N times; depth without extra parameters.
- Deep supervision — a loss on every loop step.
- DEQ — solve h* = f(h*, x) for a fixed point instead of stacking layers.
- SCF — the iterate-until-stable loop inside DFT; physics' own fixed-point iteration.
- DFT / PBE / r2SCAN — quantum simulation producing most labels; PBE cheap, r2SCAN costlier and more accurate ("label fidelity").
- MLIP — model predicting energy and forces from atom positions; the field's foundation models (MACE-MP, CHGNet, MatterSim, UMA, orb).
- CSP — predict the 3D crystal from a formula or molecule; scored by match rate.
- Best-of-N / test-time compute — sample many, rank with a verifier.
- S.U.N. / mSUN — share of generated crystals that are Stable, Unique, Novel.
- E_hull — distance from thermodynamic stability; ~0 = stable.
- Matbench — 13 property tasks (312 to 132,752 samples), nested 5-fold CV; the field's "UCI/GLUE".
- TabPFN — pretrained tabular transformer predicting in-context, no training.
- GRPO / verifiable rewards — RL fine-tuning scored by a program, MLIP or DFT.
- Multi-fidelity / distillation — mix cheap and costly labels; big MLIP labels data for a small fast student.

## 12. Smart or non-obvious ideas worth not losing (<=5)
- The empty-search list is a ready-made opportunity map; the most ML-native, low-domain-knowledge items are the repeated-data scaling study and S.U.N.-vs-budget curves, both runnable on open data.
- The refinement loop + deep supervision, not the exotic architecture, drives TRM/HRM gains (ARC Prize ablation; TRIADS 134.59 -> 103.29). Try "add an outer refinement loop to an ordinary model" first.
- Materials science already has native looped computation (SCF, charge equilibration); recursion pays where the physics is a fixed point, less as a generic trick.
- Agent "edit-run-keep-if-holdout-improves" loops on Matbench work on one consumer GPU; the reusable asset is the auditable harness with a holdout check (9 of 10 confirmed), not the model.
- Source-checking exposed many conflicting headline dataset counts; a dated, source-verified dataset-card registry is itself a small useful contribution.
