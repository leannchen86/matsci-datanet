## 4. Findings
- **Direct TRM/HRM/looped applications in materials are few, tiny, recent, mostly single-author preprints, and only reach parity with fewer parameters — no "reasoning jump".** [SOURCE]
  - TRIADS (Tiwari, Zenodo, not peer reviewed, 44K-247K params): matbench_steels MAE 91.20±12.23 MPa (312 samples) vs CrabNet 107.32, MODNet 87.76 (TRIADS trails by 3.44), TPOT-Mat 79.95, dummy 229.74; expt_gap 0.3068±0.0082 eV (beats CrabNet 0.3463, MODNet 0.3327; Darwin 0.2865 better); deep supervision alone takes steels 134.59→103.29; its HRM-style variant scored 431.87, worse than the dummy.
  - SR-CGCNN (weight-tied CGCNN, 34.5% of conv params): formation energy 0.0986 vs 0.0945 eV/atom, band gap 0.4503 vs 0.4346 eV; 10k-structure subsets, single runs, no error bars; 5 steps worse than 3.
  - SMolLM (one 53K-param block looped 8x): 95.3±0.7% valid SMILES vs 87.6±0.7% for a 527K GPT on ZINC-250K (249,455); but FCD worse, no inference FLOP saving, depth fixed at training (D=1 gives 33%).
  - Lim (A*STAR, chip-packaging FEA, 10,000 pairs each, not released): TRM is never on any Pareto frontier; plain depth-recursive variants win.
  - SCORE (ESOL, 1,128 molecules): looped 0.542±0.05 vs stacked 0.533±0.04 RMSE — parity.
  - So what: the ML hype has not yet produced a convincing materials result; the field is open but unproven.
- **Verification changed the story (corrections).** [REFUTED]
  - "TRIADS does not formally cite TRM" → wrong, it cites TRM as [13]. AutoGluon 77.03 baseline is README-only → dropped.
  - SeqBattNet "RMSE 0.028/0.035 vs ~0.58/0.55" compared against the weakest baseline (BattNN); vs LSTM/GRU/Transformer (~0.07 TRI, 0.11-0.22 RT-Batt) the real gain is ~50-60%, and on NASA data a plain FNN encoder (0.0239) beats the HRM-GRU (0.0257); recursion is shallow (N=1, T=2).
  - Dramko's ~32% structure-error reduction is vs a constant-step baseline (not vs FIRE), and force accuracy degrades. Bifurcation Models uses no materials data → relabelled "adjacent". Khan KS-map relabelled direct → analogous.
  - ICAL: text claims 8/10 wins, table shows 7/10. A-Lab: earlier note "36 of 40" → Author Correction gives 36 of 57 (63%). Zhang & Yakobson: earlier "0.18→0.1554 eV" → latest mp_gap 0.1480 eV (6th overall). SCORE's low-data claim overstated (no data-size subsets run). NN-DFT "no labels" overstated.
  - Dataset sizes: ChemCoTBench 1,120 → 1,495; ChemCoTDataset ~14k → 22,000 (paper)/~20K (README); MP-20 45,229 → 45,231; Alex-MP-20 607,673 → 607,683; Alexandria ~4.5M → 5,777,914 PBE entries; GEOM 32.7M → 37M conformers; OMat24 ~101M is the train split of ~118M; OMol25 ~76M was UMA's preview (catalog: 140,641,161 train calcs, ~83M systems).
  - So what: about half of the headline claims in the hottest papers softened on a second independent read — treat single-source numbers as provisional.
- **Materials already has its own, more mature version of "iterate one module to convergence", because DFT is a fixed-point loop.** [SOURCE] DEQHNet QH9-stable 76.31→61.42 µHa (but water 10.79→36.07 worse); HamEvo 35-49% lower Hamiltonian MAE, up to 242x faster than DFT; self-consistency training: 100 labelled + ~24,900 unlabelled conformations, ethanol 160.36→75.65; DEQuify 2-layer beats 8-layer EquiformerV2 on OC20 forces (0.035 vs 0.038) but energy worse (0.498 vs 0.392); implicit MLFFs 2-5x less compute/memory. Self-Refining DFT: mixed/negative.
- **Spending more compute at inference helps crystal/molecule generation.** [SOURCE] Clari Sol@30 0.681→0.789 (0.975 at 400 samples); OMatG-IRL ~0.5 eV/atom lower energies on MP-20; Cond-CDVAE 59.3% of 3,547 experimental structures within 800 samples; ALM Match@1 45.6% vs best-of-20 83.2%; test-time training cuts force MAE 22.9→19.9 meV/Å; PXRDGen (noted, not included) 82% at 1 sample → 96% at 20.
- **RL post-training of generators gives the largest jumps.** [SOURCE] E_hull<0.1: 44.7→73.4% (Cao & Wang); S.U.N. 7.17→22.27% (PLaID++); mSUN 15.9→61.3% vs MatterGen 41.0% (Chemeleon2); MatInvent ~1,000 evaluations vs 42,000 (26x). MatSciBench: 1,340 problems, best model 75.22%, self-correction hurt 4 of 5 models.
- **Data efficiency for force fields is well mapped.** [SOURCE] Fine-tuning: 664 configs match 3,376 from scratch; 1K r2SCAN points beat >10K from scratch. Distillation: teacher tuned on 25-150 DFT structures. Synthetic pretraining: energy RMSE −63% with 25 structures (≥32x saving). Scaling exponents 0.31 (unconstrained) → 0.75 (eSEN): built-in symmetry buys data efficiency. NequIP 133 vs 133,500 structures. UMA corpus 459M structures.
- **Small-data and loop results.** [SOURCE] TabPFN-style ICL beats composition-only SOTA on 5 of 6 small Matbench tasks (perovskites worse); ICAL saves 52% evaluations vs GP; TMD TabPFN R²>0.98 from 99 structures (0.83-0.88 on binaries/ternaries). GNoME hit rate <6%→>80% (381k new stable, 736 realised); CRESt 900+ chemistries, 3,500 tests, 9.3x; Auto Research for Materials: 701 attempts, 9/10 endpoints confirmed on holdout, steels −18.6%.
- **ML side.** [SOURCE] HRM (~27M params) claimed ARC-AGI-1 40.3% but ARC Prize verified 32%/2%; the outer refinement loop, not the hierarchy, drives results (a plain transformer within ~5pp); results are transductive. TRM 5-7M params: Sudoku-Extreme 87.4%, ARC-AGI-1 44.6%, trained on ~1k examples + heavy augmentation. "Ricursive Intelligence" is a chip-design startup with no materials work. Data repetition: ~4 epochs ≈ unique data; diffusion tolerates far more repetition than AR (R_D* 512.85 vs 31.93).
- **Dataset reuse across the verified papers:** MP-20 7 papers; MD17, MD22, QM9 5 each; ANI-1x 4; Alex-MP-20 3. Recursive-model papers use small sets (Matbench steels 312, jdft2d 636, phonons 1,265, expt_gap 4,604; ZINC-250K; 10k subsets); everything else uses large DFT corpora. ## 5. Problems / gaps identified
All are negative results of literature/citation scans (absence of evidence, not proof). [SOURCE unless noted]
- No TRM/HRM-style model for crystal structure prediction, symmetry reasoning, inverse design, retrosynthesis, MLIPs or DFT surrogates (HRM 144 citers scanned; OpenAlex TRM 5 / HRM 3 citers, none materials) — hurts anyone hoping small-data reasoning models transfer.
- No looped transformer with test-time depth scaling and no ACT/early-exit halting in equivariant force fields (only DEQ solver tolerance).
- No data-constrained (repetition) scaling study for MLIPs or crystal generators; no controlled diffusion-vs-autoregressive data-efficiency study for crystals/molecules.
- No best-of-N / S.U.N.-vs-compute-budget curves beyond five papers; none for LLM crystal generators; no AlphaFold-style recycling for inorganic CSP.
- No closed loop where robot outcomes retrain a generator; no model-collapse study although InvDesFlow-AL retrains on its own outputs.
- No DEQ for crystal property prediction or periodic Hamiltonians; no in-context MLIP; no Recursive Language Model applied to materials (0 of 88 citers); TabPFN absent from Matbench leaderboards.
- Evidence quality: the direct papers are single-author, non-peer-reviewed, single-seed, sometimes without tables or code (Lim, SeqBattNet, Dramko, Bifurcation: no code) — hurts newcomers who take abstracts at face value.
- Data access: Stress10K/Warpage10K, ether0's 640,730 training problems and MatterSim's ~17M structures are not released; CSD (>1.4M) is restricted/commercial; GNoME is CC BY-NC; ChemCoTBench licensing conflicting; many classic sets (MD17, MD22, ISO17, ESOL, USPTO-50K) state no license.

## 10. Jargon that matters for the big picture
- TRM / HRM — tiny networks that re-apply the same block many times to refine an answer; like an RNN over "thinking steps".
- Looped / weight-tied transformer — one layer reused N times instead of N distinct layers (ALBERT-style sharing, depth as a loop).
- Deep supervision / ACT halting — loss applied at every loop step / learned rule for when to stop looping.
- DEQ (deep equilibrium model) — network defined as the fixed point of a layer; "infinite depth, solve instead of stack".
- DFT / SCF loop — the physics solver that iterates electron density until self-consistent; the field's native fixed-point iteration.
- MLIP (machine-learned interatomic potential / force field) — a regressor from atom positions to energy and forces.
- CSP (crystal structure prediction) — given a formula, generate the atomic arrangement; evaluated by match rate.
- S.U.N. / mSUN — share of generated crystals that are Stable, Unique and Novel.
- E_hull — energy above the stability hull; lower = more likely to exist.
- Test-time compute — sampling more, searching or fine-tuning at inference (best-of-N analogue).
- Equivariance — symmetry built into the architecture; acts as a strong data-efficiency prior.
- Multi-fidelity (PBE vs r2SCAN) — cheap vs expensive labels for the same structures; transfer between them.
- Matbench — 13 small-to-medium property-prediction tasks with a leaderboard (the "UCI/GLUE" of materials).
- TabPFN / in-context learning — pretrained tabular model that predicts from a few hundred rows without training.
- Distillation / synthetic pretraining — big model labels cheap structures to train a small fast one.

## 12. Smart or non-obvious ideas worth not losing
- The thing that works in HRM/TRM is the outer refine-the-answer loop with supervision at every step, not the architecture; TRIADS reproduces this on 312-sample steels (134.59→103.29 from deep supervision alone). [SOURCE]
- Recursive tiny models are naturally a small-data method (~1k examples + augmentation), which matches experimental materials datasets better than the big-DFT regime. [OPINION]
- TabPFN beats composition SOTA on 5/6 small Matbench tasks yet is not on the Matbench leaderboard — a cheap, checkable contribution is implied though not proposed in the text. [SOURCE for facts; implication is the digester's reading]
- Missing "scaling-law style" studies (data repetition, diffusion vs AR, best-of-N vs budget) are analysis projects an ML person can do on public data without lab access. [OPINION, implied]
