## 10. Jargon that matters for the big picture
- Looped / weight-tied transformer — same layer reused N times (an RNN over depth); fewer params, more compute.
- HRM / TRM — tiny (5-27M) recursive reasoning nets trained on ~1k puzzles; gains come mostly from the refinement loop.
- Deep supervision — loss applied at every loop step, not just the last.
- ACT / early exit — model learns when to stop looping.
- DEQ (deep equilibrium model) — output defined as a fixed point h*=f(h*,x), solved iteratively.
- SCF (self-consistent field) — DFT's own fixed-point loop; why physics models "loop naturally".
- MLIP — ML interatomic potential: fast surrogate for DFT energies/forces; foundation MLIPs (MACE-MP, UMA, MatterSim) are its "pretrained backbones".
- Level of theory — the DFT approximation that produced labels (PBE, r2SCAN, ωB97M); label-noise/domain-shift source.
- CSP — crystal structure prediction (composition → atomic arrangement).
- S.U.N. — stable, unique, novel fraction of generated crystals; the generative-model metric.
- E_hull — energy above convex hull; stability proxy (lower = more stable).
- Best-of-N / test-time compute — sample many candidates, keep best by a verifier/MLIP.
- TabPFN / ICL — pretrained tabular transformer that predicts from the labelled set in-context, no training.
- Matbench — 13-task property benchmark (steels 312 rows → ~132k); the "GLUE" of materials property prediction.
- Distillation of foundation MLIPs — big teacher labels cheap synthetic structures; small fast student.

## 12. Smart or non-obvious ideas worth not losing (<=5)
- The credible "recursion" story in materials is not TRM clones but physics that already loops (SCF, charge equilibration, relaxation); DEQ/implicit MLIPs (DEQuify, I-MLFF) are the only direct hits and have mixed energy/force results — an obvious place for careful, seeded benchmarking.
- The strongest unfilled ML-style study is a data-repetition / epochs-vs-unique-data scaling law for MLIPs (Muennighoff-style) — all needed data (MPtrj, OMat24, SPICE) is open; nobody has done it per this search.
- The "direct" recursive papers are tiny (44K-247K params), have MIT code and run on Matbench-size data — cheap to reproduce and stress-test with multiple seeds; existing claims shrank on second reading (TRIADS trails MODNet; SeqBattNet loses to an FNN on NASA; Lim finds TRM never Pareto-optimal).
- Second full-text verification changed many headline numbers (A-Lab 41/58→36/57, ICAL 8/10→7/10, four dataset sizes) — abstract-level numbers in this field are unreliable; a "numbers-as-verified" catalog is itself a contribution.
- ARC Prize's ablation lesson (loop/refinement and augmentation matter more than the fancy hierarchy; plain transformer within ~5pp) is a transferable prior: test the simple weight-tied + deep-supervision baseline first.
