## 4. Findings (each: claim — numbers/evidence — [STATUS] — plain-language "so what")
- Related materials work exists, but true looped/TRM-style transfers are few and small — 110 papers, 68 labelled "direct" (the ML technique itself on materials/chemistry data); only 11 actually loop a network (9 in the looped group, one of them on proteins, + 2 deep-equilibrium force fields). Rest of "direct": data efficiency 18, reasoning+RL 14, small-data loops 14, test-time compute 11 — [SOURCE/OPINION labels] — the hyped architecture has barely reached materials.
- Looped models match or trail conventional ones; pitch is fewer parameters, not better accuracy — SMolLM: 53K-param weight-shared block 95.3% valid SMILES on ZINC-250K vs 87.6% for a 527K unshared GPT, but a 3.2M unshared GPT still leads (99.4%); TRIADS Matbench steels MAE 91.20±12.23 MPa vs MODNet 87.76 (CrabNet 107.32), non-peer-reviewed, AI-assisted; SR-CGCNN slightly worse on every task (MP formation energy 0.0986 vs 0.0945 eV/atom) with conv params 66,432→22,912, single runs on 10k subsets; Lim's TRM variants never Pareto-optimal (no data/code) — [SOURCE].
- No TRM/HRM-style work on crystal structure prediction (CSP), interatomic potentials (MLIPs) or retrosynthesis — OpenAlex: 5 TRM citers, 3 HRM citers, none in materials; of HRM's 144 Semantic Scholar citers only SeqBattNet qualified; Huginn's 361 citers: only SMolLM and LatentChem — [SOURCE, search result] — largest but riskiest white space.
- Materials already "loops" for physical reasons — 16 of 18 fixed-point papers iterate physical quantities (learned DFT self-consistency, Hamiltonians, charge equilibration); only DEQuify (2025) and I-MLFF (2026) loop hidden features. I-MLFF at one layer call: MD17/MD22 force MAE 0.37/0.39 vs explicit PaiNN 0.75/0.84 kcal/mol/Å, 2–5x less compute, ~1.1–1.2 solver iterations. DEQuify: OC20-200k force MAE 0.035 vs 0.038 eV/Å but energy worse (0.498 vs 0.392 eV), MD17 ~15% more accurate and ~19% faster — [SOURCE].
- Spending more compute at inference is more active than looping — Clari CSP blind test 5 success 0.681 (30 samples) → 0.789 (150) → 0.975 (400); AtomMOF steering (16 particles) formation-energy error 0.127→0.020 eV/atom, validity 81.7%→91.7%; OXtal and FRIGID improve log-linearly with samples/compute; Cond-CDVAE finds 59.3% of 3,547 test structures within 800 samples — [SOURCE].
- Data efficiency is the largest thread (25 papers) — label savings: ~5x frozen MACE-MP transfer (664 configs match 3,376 from scratch); >10x CHGNet PBE→r2SCAN (1K beats >10K); 30x MatterSim on water (30 vs 900 configs); ≥32x synthetic pretraining (25 DFT structures match 800); JMP-L fine-tune ~275 GPU-h vs ~3,300 from scratch; symmetry raises data-scaling exponents 0.31/0.39/0.50/0.75 (Ngo & Ravanbakhsh, OMol25 34M subset). NequIP's "1000x fewer than DeepMD" compares two different models, not pretraining vs scratch — [SOURCE].
- Small-data in-context learners and agent loops work now — TabPFN (no training) beats composition-only SOTA on 5 of 6 Matbench tasks (phonons 41.95 vs 46.58 cm⁻¹; worse on perovskites; not tested on steels); TabPFN-CheMeleon wins 50 of 58 Polaris+MoleculeACE tasks; TabPFN active learning fewest evaluations on 7 of 10 datasets (paper text says 8), mean savings 52% vs GP; a Claude Code research loop reached Matbench mp_gap MAE 0.1480±0.003 eV (6th overall, rediscovers known ideas); DeepSeek agent on Matbench: 701 attempts, MAE −17.4% expt_gap, −18.6% steels — [SOURCE].
- Reasoning/RL — ether0 70% reaction prediction after 46,000 examples vs 64.1% for Molecular Transformer on ~480k (different datasets; training set not released); Chemeleon2 mSUN 15.9%→61.3% on Alex-MP-20 (MatterGen 41.0%), collapses without diversity reward; PLaID++ S.U.N. 7.17%→22.27%; MatInvent converges in ~1,000 evaluations vs 42,000 DFT labels for MatterGen conditioning — [SOURCE].

## 10. Jargon that matters for the big picture
- TRM / HRM — tiny networks that re-apply the same block many times to refine an answer (weight-tied depth).
- Looped / weight-tied transformer — one layer reused N times instead of N distinct layers.
- DEQ (deep equilibrium model) — run the layer until its output stops changing; "infinite depth, one layer".
- SCF (self-consistent field) — the fixed-point loop inside DFT; physics' own recursion.
- MLIP — ML interatomic potential: a learned, fast force/energy simulator replacing DFT.
- Foundation MLIP (MACE-MP, CHGNet, UMA, MatterSim) — pretrained potentials you fine-tune, like a pretrained backbone.
- CSP — crystal structure prediction: composition in, 3D crystal out.
- S.U.N. — share of generated crystals that are stable, unique and novel; the generator quality metric.
- Best-of-N / test-time compute — sample many candidates, keep the verifier's favourite.
- Matbench — 13-task materials property benchmark (the field's small GLUE); steels task has 312 rows.
- TabPFN — pretrained tabular model that predicts in-context with no training.
- r2SCAN vs PBE — more vs less accurate DFT settings; "label fidelity".
- Multi-fidelity / cross-functional transfer — pretrain on cheap labels, fine-tune on few accurate ones.
- RLVR / GRPO — RL against programmatic verifiers instead of human labels.
- E_hull (energy above hull) — stability score; lower means more likely to exist.

## 12. Smart or non-obvious ideas worth not losing (<=5)
- Materials science had "looped networks" long before ML because its physics is a fixed-point iteration; the unexploited bridge is learned acceleration of that loop (SCF mixing), not importing TRM wholesale.
- Evaluation rules for any looped-model claim: compare to a same-size non-looped model; show depth scaling by looping ONE trained model more at test time; avoid per-instance ID embeddings that cannot generalise.
- Cheapest publishable items are measurement studies, not new models: S.U.N.-vs-budget curves and a data-repetition study on scarce high-fidelity labels (MP-r2SCAN).
- TabPFN on Matbench steels is an untested, near-zero-cost baseline sitting directly next to the user's known steels label-noise finding.
- Numbers quoted in a fast first answer were wrong in 13 places once checked at source — dataset sizes quoted in papers are often train splits or outdated; always check the source.
