# principles measurement-science

- P1 Say exactly what is being measured: A test is only valid for a stated task and range. Naming the phases (identification) and giving their amounts (quantification) are different tasks, and the old contests kept them apart on purpose.
  CHECK: Does the eval card state, one line each: task (name phases only, or name plus amounts), naming level required, wt% range covered, sample type (weighed mixture or real product), and instrument or radiation? If any line is blank, fail.

[memory] https://www.eurachem.org/index.php/publications/guides/mv
- P2 The answer key needs its own check: A weighed recipe is a claim, not the truth. Each ingredient must be checked for purity, and the mixed recipe must be confirmed by an independent measurement, with an error bar on every weight fraction.
  CHECK: For every mixture, is there (a) a scan of each pure ingredient showing no second phase above a stated limit, (b) one independent check of the mix (bulk chemistry such as XRF, or a second analysis method), and (c) a stated uncertainty on each wt%? Three yes answers, or fail.

- P3 No ranking inside the key's error: If two entrants differ by less than the uncertainty of the answer key (plus sampling error), call it a tie. Proficiency-test statistics widen the score when the key itself is uncertain.
  CHECK: Does the tie function take two inputs, sampling error from the number of items AND a stated key error rate? Does any 'A beats B' sentence in the write-up survive both? If the key error is not stated, fail.

- P4 Prove the test items are uniform and stable: Before items are used, show that splits of the same powder agree with each other and that the powder does not change between mixing and scanning.
  CHECK: Is there a written homogeneity check (re-scan or re-split a random subset, compare against a preset limit) and a stability rule (date mixed, date scanned, air-sensitive or water-absorbing ingredients flagged)? Yes or no.

- P5 Separate reading a scan from making one: Test the analysis step on fixed, supplied scans, and test sample preparation plus measurement separately. One instrument gives repeatability only; it says nothing about other instruments.
  CHECK: Does every headline number carry the label 'fixed scans, one instrument'? Is there a named, sized second-instrument subset planned before any claim that a tool 'works on lab XRD'? If the claim is general and the data is one instrument, fail.

- P6 Put known physical traps in on purpose: The field knows which physical effects break powder XRD. A credible test includes items built to trigger each one, labels them, and reports results per label.
  CHECK: Does each item carry tags for: strong absorption contrast between ingredients, plate- or needle-shaped ingredient, non-crystalline ingredient, poorly crystalline ingredient, grain size known? Is there at least one item per tag, and a per-tag score? If the only difficulty axis is counting noise, fail.
  SRC: IUCr Commission on Powder Diffraction 1998 Quantitative Phase Analysis Round Robin: letter to participants [opened] https://www.iucr.org/resources/commissions/powder-diffraction/projects/qarr/data/letter; Scarlett et al. 2002 Outcomes of the IUCr CPD Round Robin on QPA: samples 2, 3, 4, synthetic bauxite, natural granodiorite and pharmaceuticals [opened] https://journals.iucr.org/paper?S0021889802008798; Brindley 1945 The effect of grain or particle size on X-ray reflections from mixed powders and alloys (Phil. - P7 Report detection by amount, with a floor: Give the hit rate as a curve against how much of the phase is present, in bands fixed in advance. Mark the band below the instrument's physical floor as 'not expected to be visible', not as a tool failure.
  CHECK: Are wt% bands fixed before running, does each band show item count and an interval, and is there a declared floor (with its source) below which misses are not counted against the tool? Three yes answers, or fail.
  SRC: León-Reina, García-Maté, Álvarez-Pinazo, Santacruz, Vallcorba, De la Torre, Aranda 2016 Accuracy in Rietveld quantitative phase analysis: a comparative study of strictly monochromatic Mo and Cu radiations (J. - P8 Wrong extra phases cost as much as misses: Automated search tools over-report. The contest community found that naming phases that are not there is the largest single error source, so the score must punish it directly.
  CHECK: Does the score sheet show a separate false-positive count per scan (not only recall or a blended F1)? Does the exam include single-phase items and items where the right answer is a short list? If precision is not reported on its own, fail.
  SRC: Clay Minerals Society 2026 (page as read) The Reynolds Cup (rules and guidelines page) [opened] https://www.clays.org/reynolds/; Raven and Self 2017 Outcomes of 12 years of the Reynolds Cup quantitative mineral analysis round robin (Clays and Clay Minerals 65, 122-134) [opened] https://link.springer.com/article/10.1346/CCMN.2017.064054; Lutterotti, Pillière, Fontugne, Boullay, Chateigner 2019 Full-profile search-match by the Rietveld method (J. - P9 Publish naming levels before the test: Decide in advance which answers count as the same (same family, same series, same structure type), with a number for every tolerance. Also list what a powder scan cannot distinguish at all.
  CHECK: Is there a grouping table, frozen before any scoring, with a numeric tolerance per tier, plus a list of pair types declared unanswerable from a lab powder scan? If grading choices are made after seeing answers, fail.

- P10 Fix and log what the entrant was given: Results depend heavily on the reference database version and on hints such as which elements are present. Record both for every run and never pool results across them.
  CHECK: Does each result row record: reference database name and date, whether the element list was given, and whether every true phase was in the candidate list? Are scores reported separately for each setting? If any of the three is missing, fail.
  SRC: Le Meins, Cranswick, Le Bail 2002 Search-Match Round Robin 2002: results page [opened] https://www.cristal.org/smrr/results/; Lutterotti, Pillière, Fontugne, Boullay, Chateigner 2019 Full-profile search-match by the Rietveld method [opened] https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2019/03/00/nb5231/; IUCr Commission on Powder Diffraction 1998 Quantitative Phase Analysis Round Robin: letter to participants [opened] https://www.iucr.org/resources/commissions/powder-diffraction/projects/qarr/data/letter
- P11 Freeze settings; the operator is a variable: Round robins found that who runs the software, and with what settings, moves the result more than which method is used. A tool's score is really the score of tool plus settings plus operator.
  CHECK: Is each tool run from a frozen, published configuration with no per-scan human adjustment, and is the configuration recorded with the score? Is it flagged when the tool's authors could have tuned on the same public scans? If a human touched individual scans, fail.
  SRC: Madsen, Scarlett, Cranswick, Lwin 2001 Outcomes of the IUCr CPD Round Robin on QPA: samples 1a to 1h [opened] https://journals.iucr.org/paper?S0021889801007476; Scarlett et al. 2002 Outcomes of the IUCr CPD Round Robin on QPA: samples 2, 3, 4, synthetic bauxite, natural granodiorite and pharmaceuticals [opened] https://journals.iucr.org/paper?S0021889802008798; Lutterotti, Pillière, Fontugne, Boullay, Chateigner 2019 Full-profile search-match by the Rietveld method [opened] https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2019/03/00/nb5231/
- P12 Reproduce a certified answer before building anything: Run the whole pipeline first on an item whose answer was certified or contest-verified by someone else. If it cannot reproduce that answer, nothing built on top can be trusted.
  CHECK: Has the marker-plus-tool pipeline been run on at least one externally verified item (for example the round robin's supplied scans of the three-phase sample, or a scan of a certified reference mixture) and returned the known phases? Yes or no.
  SRC: NIST page as read 2026 Cement characteristics: direct phase assessment (programme page) [opened] https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/cement-characteristics-direct-phase-assessment; Instrument vendor application note hosted by AZoM undated web page Clinker phase analysis checked against ASTM C1365 limits [opened] https://www.azom.com/article.aspx?ArticleID=23379; Lutterotti, Pillière, Fontugne, Boullay, Chateigner 2019 Full-profile search-match by the Rietveld method [opened] https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2019/03/00/nb5231/; Cline, Von Dreele, Winburn, Stephens, Filliben (NIST) 2011 Certification of NIST SRM 676a [opened] https://www.nist.gov/publications/addressing-amorphous-content-issue-quantitative-phase-analysis-certification-nist-srm
- P13 Weighed mixtures are the easy case: Real reaction products contain mixed-composition phases, unknown phases and poorly crystalline material that no weighed mixture has. Accuracy on weighed mixtures is an upper bound, and keys for real samples need an appeal path.
  CHECK: Does the write-up call weighed-mixture accuracy an upper bound in plain words? Is there a planned second arm on real products, scored against expert consensus, with a written way to challenge and revise the key? If the weighed exam is presented as 'real-world accuracy', fail.
  SRC: Scarlett et al. 2002 Outcomes of the IUCr CPD Round Robin on QPA: samples 2, 3, 4, synthetic bauxite, natural granodiorite and pharmaceuticals [opened] https://journals.iucr.org/paper?S0021889802008798; Le Meins, Cranswick, Le Bail 2002 Search-Match Round Robin 2002: results page [opened] https://www.cristal.org/smrr/results/; Lutterotti, Pillière, Fontugne, Boullay, Chateigner 2019 Full-profile search-match by the Rietveld method [opened] https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2019/03/00/nb5231/
- P14 Fresh items each round, key holder stays out: Standing schemes stay honest by making new items every round, releasing the truth only after closing, limiting entries, hiding entrant identities, and keeping the person who holds the key out of the contest.
  CHECK: Is there a written rule for each of: item retirement once truth is released; who holds the key and that they do not enter; entries allowed per entrant per round; entrant codes instead of names; nothing in file names or metadata that leaks the recipe? Any missing rule, fail.

## prior art
- Reynolds Cup (Clay Minerals Society) [opened] in_notes=True: A free, biennial, blind contest since 2002. Three weighed mineral mixtures per round, about 100 sample sets, no phase list given, any method allowed. Score is the sum of absolute wt% errors; ties inside the key's uncertainty go to tie-breaks that count missed and wrongly named phases. Truth released after closing. 448 participants and about 14,000 analyses over 2002-2014.
  OVERLAPS: L2 (hidden-recipe exam on weighed mixtures); D4 (tie rule); N1/D1 (grouping levels fixed in advance); G17/G20 in gaps.md
  OPEN: It is for human analysts, on clay-bearing rocks, three items every two years, one entry per organisation. No machine-facing submission or public scoring code was seen. Not inorganic-synthesis chemistry. New to the notes: the rules detail (purity within 1 wt%, XRD+XRF split checks, published grouping spreadsheet, tie-break inside key uncertainty, organiser does not compete).
  https://www.clays.org/reynolds/
- Past Reynolds Cup mixtures on sale [opened] in_notes=True: The society's store lists past contest reference mixtures at $250 per unit, 2-4 g each, limit of 4 units, several past rounds listed.
  OVERLAPS: plan.md DON'T DO row (buying past contest powders on one unverified price); G20
  OPEN: The price is now verified on the seller's own page, so 'unverified' can be dropped. The don't-buy call still stands for a different reason: few items, clay-heavy, and their compositions were released to entrants after each round, so they are not hidden. Whether full keys are posted publicly was not checked.
  https://cms.clays.org/source-clay/
- Butler and Hillier 2021, automated full-pattern summation (R package powdR) scored on Reynolds Cup samples [opened] in_notes=False: An automatic algorithm that picks the phases itself from a 201-pattern library, run on 27 samples from nine past contests. Mean absolute error 0.57 wt% for non-clay minerals, 2.37 for clays, 4.43 for non-crystalline phases. Would have placed in the top three in all nine contests.
  OVERLAPS: L2 novelty claim (machine-scored on blind-contest weighed mixtures); N3 (practice exam); G20 conclusion
  OPEN: Abstract only was read. It is a one-off, run by the tool's authors after the truth was public, with their own library, on minerals, scored on amounts not on naming. It shows 'a machine scored against contest weighed mixtures' already exists. It does not give a standing, hidden, third-party exam.
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42860-020-00105-6
- Lutterotti et al. 2019, full-profile search-match (FPSM) tested on round-robin scans [opened] in_notes=False: A fully automatic phase-naming and quantifying tool with a public web form, tested on IUCr round-robin data set 1h (within 2% absolute of weighed values) and on all four samples of the 2002 search-match round robin. One sample failed because the structure was not in the open database; on another the tool disagreed with the organisers' key.
  OVERLAPS: N3 and D6 (re-using public round-robin scans as a machine practice exam); P12 positive control; G14/G17
  OPEN: Five scans, authors' own run, no written marking rule, no repeat. Confirms the round-robin scans were publicly downloadable from the IUCr site in 2019 and have already been used as development examples, so they are contaminated as an unseen test.
  https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2019/03/00/nb5231/
- IUCr CPD round robin on quantitative phase analysis (Madsen 2001; Scarlett 2002) [opened] in_notes=True: 1998-2002 interlaboratory test. Eight three-phase weighed mixtures plus samples built to trigger preferred orientation, non-crystalline content and microabsorption, a 7-phase synthetic bauxite, a natural rock with no key, and two drug mixtures. Key cross-checked by XRF. Separate arms for supplied scans and own scans. Scored with a concentration-weighted Kullback-Leibler distance.
  OVERLAPS: D6 and N3 (harder public weighed sets); G20; L2 design
  OPEN: New to the notes: phase identities were GIVEN to entrants, so it never tested naming; weighed values have been public since 1999; only the supplied-data arm is a fixed scan set; roughly 20 scans in total. Usable as a positive control and a small practice set, not as a hidden exam.
  https://journals.iucr.org/paper?S0021889801007476
- Search-Match Round Robin 2002 (Le Meins, Cranswick, Le Bail) [opened] in_notes=True: The only phase-NAMING round robin found. Four real samples, two steps (without and with chemistry), about 25-30 entrants, nine software packages. One entrant found all ten phases. Grading was lenient and done by hand: same-series members were accepted.
  OVERLAPS: D1 and N1 (marking rule, 'same family' tier); G14
  OPEN: New detail: database age alone failed about 16% of entrants on one sample; one phase was missing from the database; the key was later challenged (see FPSM 2019). No written rule card, four items, and no repeat round was found (one search; absence not proven).
  https://www.cristal.org/smrr/results/
- León-Reina et al. 2016 spiking series and its Zenodo record [opened] in_notes=True: 78 careful analyses of mixtures spiked with 0.12 to 4.0 wt% of a minor phase (inorganic, organic and glass series), lab Cu and Mo sources plus synchrotron. Record is CC BY 4.0, about 4.8 MB, raw scans in vendor formats (.raw, .xrdml) and .XY.
  OVERLAPS: D6 (one of the three harder public sets); N3 wt% bands; G20; G28
  OPEN: Landing page and paper abstract only; nothing downloaded. Licence and size are now verified. Much of the range sits near the physical detection floor (about 0.2 wt%; good accuracy only above 1 wt%), and phase identities are known by design, so it measures a detection curve, not open naming.
