# materials-ml-evals

## is eval work: mostly
EVAL: D1 (testing a scoring rule, plus a unit-test file for it). N1 (the scoring function and re-marking runner). D3 and N2 (train/test splits, leakage, a frozen test list, baselines). D4 (statistics of comparing two scores). D6 and N3 (finding and running a practice test set). N4 (best achievable score given label noise). N5 (a short guide on how to read a leaderboard). L1 (validating an answer key). L2 and L5 (building a hidden test). About two thirds of the items, and all of the first-week recommendation.
NOT: D0 is housekeeping (file rescue). D2 is a dataset provenance audit: data curation that serves evaluation (it protects the 'measured' label) but is not itself a test, split or metric. D5, L3 and L4 are error reports to dataset owners. N6 is data-quality flagging. The earlier arithmetic on one company's 134-item test is critique that borrows evaluation statistics. Note also what kind of eval work this is: almost all of it audits existing tests and the user's own harness (meta-evaluation). Only L2 builds a new test, and that is months away and needs a lab.
NAME: Benchmark auditing for ML on measured materials data: check the splits, scoring rules, answer keys and error bars of the tests that exist, before building a new one.

## direction findings
- [low] The overall direction is right. It matches where the materials-ML community's own criticism has gone, and the measured-data side has no equivalent yet.
  EVID: The best-known benchmark redesign in the field (Matbench Discovery) is built on forward-in-time test sets, decision metrics, de-duplication and a training-data rule. A 2025 materials paper treats evaluation as measurement and proposes evaluation cards. A 2025 study shows most held-out-group tests are interpolation. All three are for computed data. The plan already passes or nearly passes 5 of 14 checks (P3, P8, P12, P13 follow; P1 nearly) and its cautions in plan/plan.md sections 7 and 8 are mostly acted on. Sources from the stage-1 compile, not re-opened by me.
  REC: Keep the direction. Use those three papers as the template a reviewer will hold the work to, and say so in N5.
- [high] Step one checks the user's marker against itself. It should be anchored to the original authors' per-scan marks, which exist.
  EVID: plan/plan.md:33 vs the Dara paper (opened this run): the rule is written ('includes all precursor phases ... excludes any spurious phases') and per-scan summaries are said to be released as two spreadsheets.
