Working protocol v0.1

Research question: Which experimental details and recipe-to-result relationships are recoverable from the Stanford AlN main article, what does its supplement add, and which links remain unsupported?

PurposeProduce a checkable reconstruction that shows what an experimental-data system can recover faithfully and what it must leave unknown.
SourcesOne fixed version of the public article and its appended supplement, including figures/captions. No other papers, private records or outside knowledge may fill an answer in this first pass.
UnitOne question about one identified object: a campaign, condition group, specimen or measurement. A campaign pressure range and a particular specimen’s pressure are different questions.
Question set16 questions frozen in version 0.1 before structured extraction and any model scoring. They cover deposition, substrate, temperature, thickness, characterization, uncertainty and recipe–result links. This is a purposive case study, not a representative sample of all research.
ProcedureBuild a main-article evidence column first, then add supplement evidence without overwriting it. Attach page/section/figure, units and object identity to each answer. We have already read both sources; this is an information-location audit, not a blinded human experiment.
EvidenceExplicit; partial; inferred; not found within these sources; or conflicting/ambiguous. Label exact values, ranges and figure estimates separately. Never join conditions to outcomes merely because their values look similar.
OutputsSource-linked evidence table; map of supported recipe → specimen → measurement → result links; short findings note. Use a script for reproducible counts if useful.
AssessmentCount questions supported by the main article, newly resolved by the supplement, and still unresolved. Report ambiguities separately and name unsupported links. Every count must be inspectable at question level.
StopComplete the fixed table and a verification pass. Little added information or incomplete run-level reconstruction is a valid result. Do not expand sources or questions simply to obtain a stronger finding.
ClaimWhat can be recovered from these particular public sources. No claim yet about unpublished archives, training gains, faster experiments, laboratory reproducibility or demand.

Optional later model comparison

The scientific evidence audit is the primary deliverable. After the reference answers are verified, this secondary test can ask whether additional supplied context improves reconstruction.

TaskRecover experimental fields and their evidence, including when a requested linkage is not disclosed.
AMain paper supplied to the model.
BThe same paper plus supplement, with identical questions and instructions.
ControlsSame model/settings/format; fresh contexts; browsing disabled; questions frozen before runs; all outputs retained.
ReferenceHuman-checked evidence table with exact locations. Ambiguous questions remain explicitly unscored.
MeasuresSupported correct answers, justified abstentions, wrong/unsupported answers and invented links. Correctness and support are separate.
ResultDescriptive counts plus source-checkable examples; no predetermined winner.
LimitSupplied-context reconstruction, not model training or faster science. Prior model knowledge remains possible.

Stop or revise when

  • No run-level records are recoverable: produce a claim/field ledger instead of inventing rows.
  • Parsing errors or unequal instructions drive the result: repair and disclose the rerun.
  • A finding restates the paper: call it reconstruction, not discovery.
  • Extra context gives no useful improvement: report that result and finish the case.
  • A correct answer lacks support in the supplied source: score the support failure separately.
  • Human verification remains uncertain: retain the ambiguity; AI agreement is not resolution.

This can demonstrate careful reading, provenance and reproducible analysis. Whether authors find it useful or want to collaborate remains unverified.
