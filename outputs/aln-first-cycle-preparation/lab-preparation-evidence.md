# Practical preparation for the first Stanford AlN cycle

Checked 4 October 2026. Primary route: hands-on work after Stanford's required access and qualification. These preparations can start before registration finishes. No stock availability, reservation, purchase or staff acceptance is established here. Use the existing [scope](research-scope.md) and [traveler](lab-traveler-template.md); this checklist does not add experiments.

## Prepare now

- [ ] Prepare one substrate specification sheet: Si(100), approximately 14 nm **thermal** SiO₂, proposed coupon dimensions, wafer thickness and finish, supplier/lot, oxide-thickness evidence, and all known prior processing. Have deposition, X-ray and TDTR owners accept the dimensions before ordering or cutting.
- [ ] Assign one run ID and coupon IDs A/B/(C). All receive AlN in the same deposition. A receives the Al transducer after thickness/texture measurements; B is the spare with **AlN but no Al transducer**; optional C is another same-run reserve. These are not independent deposition repeats.
- [ ] Prepare a carrier-position sketch and an electronic materials/history sheet. Include dicing adhesive, mounting materials, prior tools and any existing cleaning. Ask staff to select the accepted cleaning and handling route; do not adopt an improvised chemical recipe.
- [ ] Use the traveler to name each handoff, operator, coupon, file and next action. Keep the supplier certificate, specimen photographs, original instrument exports and analysis outputs linked by coupon/run ID. Record unknowns explicitly.

## Resolve these physical details before buying or processing

**Outside samples and tool compatibility.** Submit the stack and processing history for staff review. Stanford's tool classes depend on materials and prior processing; analytical equipment does not restore an earlier cleanliness classification. A new purchased wafer is not by itself evidence of compatibility with every tool. Ask whether the proposed route uses an existing approved process or needs a new-process/material request. Do not interpret historical approvals for other projects as this project's approval. [Cleanliness groups](https://snfguide.stanford.edu/guide/materials/cleanliness-groups-for-process-flows); [PROM process](https://snfguide.stanford.edu/guide/materials/new-process-or-material-requests-prom).

**Cleaning.** General-use chemicals are stocked; wet-bench work requires training/qualification. New chemicals require approval and registered storage. Have the trainer specify the actual preparation and its record; do not buy or bring cleaning chemicals in advance. [SNF manual, §§4.4, 5.8](https://snfguide.stanford.edu/snf-lab-manual).

**Carrier and mounting.** Both Lesker manuals describe pieces attached to full 4- or 6-inch substrates. Confirm who supplies the carrier, usable area, attachment, coupon spacing and thermal contact. Their Kapton allowance excludes exposure to sputter etching/elevated temperatures, so it is not blanket approval for this run. Have the trainer accept the mount before buying tape or a custom holder. [Lesker manual](https://snfguide.stanford.edu/guide/docs/operating-instructions/lesker-operation-manual); [Lesker2 manual](https://snfguide.stanford.edu/guide/docs/operating-instructions/lesker2-operation-manual).

**Cutting.** Supplier-cut coupons may avoid an extra facility step; obtain holder acceptance first. Stanford's NPC lists a FlipScribe for wafers up to 4 inches, with cleaving tools/holders supplied. Its page requires NPC membership/access but says no further tool training is required for this instrument; follow its local SOP and obtain staff assistance if needed. It still refers to Badger, so confirm the current access route. This is an optional resource, not proof that Allen/SNF onboarding grants NPC access. [NPC general equipment and SOP links](https://snsf.stanford.edu/facilities/fab/npc/general).

**Target timing and temperature records.** The Lesker2 manual publishes a two-week target-change cadence and at least six hours of pump-down after a change; confirm current practice and installed Al early. Both manuals request parameter logging, but do not establish an exportable actual-coupon-temperature record. Before the run, agree what the instrument records, where temperature is sensed, whether the value is a setpoint/stage measurement/coupon measurement/estimate, and how the below-200°C condition will be supported. Do not equate no active heating with a measured temperature limit. The manual's older Badger/trainer references make current confirmation necessary. [Lesker2 manual](https://snfguide.stanford.edu/guide/docs/operating-instructions/lesker2-operation-manual).

## Read before the in-person session

- [ ] SNF manual sections cited above; cleanliness groups and PROM route.
- [ ] Manual for the staff-selected Lesker tool, followed by the [current Lesker training instructions](https://snfguide.stanford.edu/guide/equipment/training/sputter-lesker-12-training). Reading prepares for qualification; it does not replace it.
- [ ] Ask the trainer to demonstrate approved mounting and the existing recipe/log export before the first run. Capture intended versus observed values, units, timestamps, mounting, deviations and available temperature evidence. Add no new sensors or tool modifications without the equipment owner's acceptance.

The purchasing decision needs four concrete answers: accepted wafer/coupon specification, approved carrier/mount, current Al target/process availability, and which handling items the facility supplies or sells. Resolve those before ordering a broad consumables kit.
