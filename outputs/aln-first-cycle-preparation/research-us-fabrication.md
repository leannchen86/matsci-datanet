# US fabrication sourcing for the first AlN cycle

Research checked **2026-10-04**. Primary sources are linked beside the relevant claims. This is preparation for requests for feasibility and quotations; no provider has been contacted and no capability, price, slot, purchase, or booking has been agreed. Rankings below are research judgments, not evidence that any provider has accepted the exact job.

## Working scope and recommended route

**Updated user choice: hands-on, solo access to nano@stanford is the first preference; budget ceiling $15,000; desired completion October 25, 2026.** The billing entity is unresolved. The proposed job is **one deposition recipe, one batch of 2–3 Si(100)/approximately 14 nm thermal-SiO2 coupons, approximately 200 nm reactive DC sputtered AlN at low substrate temperature**, followed by film thickness and crystallographic alignment measurements, an approved Al transducer, and TDTR on one sample. Several coupons from one deposition are specimens from the same run, not independent deposition repeats.

Prioritize **Stanford external enrollment, process review, and tool qualification**. **Hionix in San Jose** is the commercial fallback if hands-on access or training misses the desired date. **Utah Nanofab staff services** are a non-California fallback with published rates. None has publicly demonstrated the precise Stanford-paper stack, equipment configuration, recipe, and resulting thermal conductivity as an available service. The October 25 date cannot yet be promised: account approval, orientation, tool training, target availability, and metrology turnaround are separate schedule dependencies.

For the selected route, the user becomes the fabrication operator after the required qualifications; expert process review remains useful before a first independent run. Prefer one measurement provider that can coordinate thickness, alignment, transducer preparation, and thermal analysis. The thermal provider should approve the stack and sample preparation before fabrication is booked.

## Stanford: preferred hands-on route

**Access is feasible without being a Stanford student.** nano@stanford accepts outside academic, government, nonprofit, and industry organizations. Its process involves an organization account, agreement, financial authorization, and purchase order. The published framework does not clearly establish a standalone unaffiliated-individual account. Ask **nano-admin-external@lists.stanford.edu** about the intended contracting entity through the current [external-access page and linked forms](https://nanolabs.stanford.edu/access/external).

Independent consultants are an optional source of process review or an operator fallback. **Facility charges go to the customer's Stanford account, while consultant fees are arranged separately**. Stanford lists Aleta Jamora (**ajsnfconsult@gmail.com**), Jerry Yang (**jerryyang747@comcast.net**), Kathy Jackson (**kj.jackson@comcast.net**), Ludwig Galambos (**ludwig.galambos@gmail.com**), and Yonas Yemane (**yonas@vector-nano.com**). ## Hionix: commercial fallback

**Verified.** Hionix offers contract R&D and serves startups. Its public service table lists **AlN and Al** as available films/targets. The table gives 25 mm as the nominal minimum silicon sample size; smaller-coupon acceptance needs confirmation. Its in-house **dry oxidation range is 500–3,000 Å, or 50–300 nm**. Therefore **14 nm oxide is outside its published standard range**; custom 14 nm growth has not been verified. Silicon wafers are offered by inquiry. Most standard film services are advertised below ten days, while R&D can take longer; this is not a lead-time commitment for this project. [Company services](https://hionix.com/), [film, substrate, oxide, and wafer specifications](https://hionix.com/pvd-films/).

Hionix describes DC and RF equipment, reactive nitride sputtering, and actively cooled substrates. Its reactive-process description says RF or pulsed DC is typical. Those statements do **not** establish that the proposed AlN run will use continuous DC. Different generic cooling configurations are advertised, but none establishes actual sample temperature during this recipe. Named metrology includes Filmetrics F50 and Siemens D5000 XRD; a suitable AlN(002) rocking-curve measurement is not explicitly established. No TDTR service was verified. [Process and metrology capabilities](https://hionix.com/process-capabilities/).

**Actual contact.** [Hionix contact form](https://hionix.com/contact/), **sales@hionix.com**, **408-232-7000**; 1980 Concourse Drive, Suite 100, San Jose, CA 95131. The form asks for film, thickness, application, substrate size/material, and quantity. Company name is not marked mandatory, but that alone does not prove that Hionix contracts with unaffiliated individuals. Confirm the contracting party, minimum order, and payment terms.

**Questions that decide whether it fits this job:**

- Can a named engineer run low-temperature reactive **continuous DC** sputtering from metallic Al in Ar/N2 on oxidized Si? If only pulsed DC, RF, or a compound target is offered, identify the difference before pricing it as the requested experiment.
- What existing AlN process experience is available on SiO2, at approximately 200 nm thickness? Is this a known recipe or development work? What evidence can be shared about orientation, uniformity, temperature, and film quality without promising the paper's conductivity?
- What coupon dimensions and carrier arrangement will be accepted for all 2–3 specimens in one batch? Can Hionix obtain the specified thermal oxide, or should approved substrates be shipped in?
- Can the operator supply measured/recorded recipe settings, temperature method, run deviations, sample positions, thickness results, and agreed raw files? An ordinary coating order may not include process disclosure.
- Which measurements can be performed to the thermal provider's required accuracy? Is AlN(002) omega rocking-curve data available, rather than only a phase scan or a generic peak width?

**Money and timing.** No public quote, minimum charge, or project-specific lead time was found. Request a line-item R&D quote, separating feasibility/engineering, materials, target/setup changes, deposition, optional metrology, packaging, and shipping. Ask for the earliest slot **after** substrate receipt and technical acceptance. Treat the website's standard-production timing as background only.

## Utah Nanofab: staff-operated backup

Utah explicitly offers staff-performed micro/nanofabrication. Its [off-campus access page](https://www.nanofab.utah.edu/off-campus-users-cleanroom-access/) directs clients to the [work-authorization form](https://workauth.cores.utah.edu/#/offcampus) and **jeffrey.farrer@utah.edu** for project discussion. The access page's role title differs from the current directory: the [staff page](https://www.nanofab.utah.edu/lab-staff/) lists Jeff Farrer with EMSAL and **Jim Pierce, jim.pierce@utah.edu**, as cleanroom process engineer. Jim is also the named sputter-tool owner and is the strongest technical contact; **amy.lash@utah.edu** handles administrative questions. Industry access is established; contracting as an unaffiliated individual needs confirmation.

The [process list](https://www.nanofab.utah.edu/processes/) includes Al/AlN and nitrogen-reactive sputtering. However, the more specific [Denton 635 page](https://www.nanofab.utah.edu/wiki/denton-635-2/) lists standard targets **Au, Cr, Pt, and Ti**, with other targets requiring staff arrangements. It supports DC/pulsed DC/RF and reactive nitrogen/oxygen. This makes **Al-target availability a real setup question**, not something established by the broad material list. Ask about current stage cooling/heating configuration, low-temperature control, target installation/conditioning, contamination history, and feasibility on the specified oxide. A target/material listing is not a validated thermally conductive AlN recipe.

Published industry rates effective July 1, 2026 are **Denton 635 $170/hour**, **Discovery 18 $160/hour**, and **professional staff $150/hour**. Thus concurrent Denton-plus-staff billing would be $320/hour arithmetically, before other charges; no required number of hours is established. Several thickness/profilometry tools are $38/hour, and the e-beam evaporators are $156/hour. Supplies and additional services can add cost. The **$300 new-member setup fee** pertains to industry members obtaining access; do not automatically apply it to a staff-only remote project. [Official July 2026 rate sheet](https://www.nanofab.utah.edu/documents/2026/06/cleanroom-rates-7-1-2026.pdf/).

There is no verified project-specific fabrication lead time or all-in AlN price. Request a staff-service quote with target changes, conditioning, fixtures, consumables, engineering time, and idle/pumpdown billing explicit. No TDTR service was verified here.

Ask each bidder to distinguish **known process execution** from **new process development**. Include all planning, target changes, pumpdown/conditioning, actual run time, sample preparation, characterization, project management, shipping, and tax where applicable. A low hourly sputter rate alone does not price the cycle. If the provider cannot disclose essential recipe records, it may still sell a coating, but that service would not satisfy this project's experimental-record objective.

## Booking decision and excluded shortcuts

The proposed booking gate is reached only when the fabrication operator confirms a feasible tool/recipe, the measurement provider accepts the stack, substrate dimensions and ownership are settled, data deliverables are written down, and a complete estimate plus operational stop-work cap is agreed. Do not promise the original paper's conductivity as the acceptance criterion for a single run. The first cycle should produce interpretable samples and a traceable experimental record even if performance is poor.

UCSD remains a relevant research lead, but this search did not establish that a public shared-facility sputter booking provides the Kummel paper's HiPIMS process or a staff-operated equivalent. [SDNI contact page](https://sdni.ucsd.edu/contact.php). It therefore ranks behind the more concrete routes above for this particular request.

UCSB's public [sputtering recipe page](https://wiki.nanofab.ucsb.edu/wiki/Sputtering_Recipes) marks Sputter 2 disabled; historical AlN recipes are not evidence of an available service. The substrate AlN mentioned on some other coating vendors' sites likewise must not be mistaken for an AlN film-deposition offering. No replacement process should silently change continuous reactive DC to HiPIMS, RF, or another chemistry.

**Remaining uncertainties across all routes:** individual/company contracting, willingness to perform this small custom job, suitable existing recipe, complete record release, precise sample temperature, real instrument availability, full price, schedule, and successful thermal-property extraction. Public capabilities support asking for feasibility; they do not resolve these questions.
