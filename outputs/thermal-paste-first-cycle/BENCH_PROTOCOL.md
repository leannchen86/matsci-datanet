### 1C. Check the balance before weighing ingredients

1. Verify its model and capacity from the label/manual; the discussed 500 g capacity is not assumed from the order conversation. The load limit includes the vessel and contents even after taring. Let the balance and materials reach the working room's temperature; warm up, level and calibrate as the actual manual specifies.
2. With clean, documented-tolerance check weights, check representative small additions and the working load range, including checks with the actual empty cup/preload on the pan. Approximately **2 g, 5 g and 20 g** are useful check masses for the staged method: the smallest fine-powder addition is 1.83 g, even though its batch total is 5.49 g. Documented weight combinations can be used with their combined tolerance/uncertainty. Handle weights without contaminating them.
3. For each selected check, place/remove the weight five times and record all five readings, not just an average. Verify zero return, stability and drift. The proposed preparation criterion from checklist item 16 is: **absolute indication error plus the check-weight tolerance/uncertainty at most 0.010 g, and max-minus-min repeated reading at most 0.010 g.** These are working criteria for this pilot, not a manufacturer's accuracy claim or a certification.
4. If readings drift, jump or fail the checks, investigate the surface, drafts, warm-up, leveling or static before preparing recipe batches. A displayed third decimal or a single supplied calibration weight does not establish performance for small additions. Without suitable check weights, rehearsal can proceed but the preparation accuracy remains unverified.
5. Record the empty mixing cup's mass without its lid. For later aliquot containers also record the empty **container plus matching lid** mass, so a sealed gross mass can be converted to net contents correctly.

### 1D. Write the plan, then make the practice batch

Print one [batch sheet](../../trajectory/batch-sheet.md). Enter batch/session IDs, cup/tool type, material lots, balance ID, room temperature/humidity if measured, target masses and planned mixing method. Record missing measurements as not measured, not estimated observations. Save any forecasts being evaluated **before** the relevant outcome; use a completed prompt matching these actual McMaster materials and whichever measurements are planned. Paper is the bench record; transcribe promptly and preserve the original. No outcome has been recorded merely by writing this procedure.

Use this fixed **approximately 25 g mass recipe**:

| Ingredient | Total target | Addition pattern |
|---|---:|---|
| 1000 cSt silicone oil | **6.71 g** | All first |
| Coarse alumina | **12.81 g** | **4.27 g** in each of three rounds |
| Fine alumina | **5.49 g** | **1.83 g** in each of three rounds |

These rounded targets sum to **25.01 g**. There is no need to remove 0.01 g to make a nominal 25 g batch. The nominal 40 vol% filler / 30% fine within filler description assumes both aluminas have true density 3.97 g/mL and the oil 0.970 g/mL. The oil value is in the [manufacturer TDS](https://www.super-lube.com/Content/Images/uploaded/documents/TDS/Technical_Data_Sheet_Silicone_Oil.pdf); the actual McMaster lots' true particle densities remain unverified. This is a declared mass recipe with an estimated volume of about 11.5 mL, not a measured volume or density. Do not use bulk powder density in this calculation.

**Each ingredient reading below is a fresh net addition, not a cumulative target:**

1. Place the clean labeled mixing cup, without lid or tool, on the balance. Tare to zero. Add oil slowly, approaching **6.71 g**. Let the display stabilize and write down the actual mass to the displayed precision. Keep oil off the cup exterior and balance pan.
2. **Round 1, coarse:** with the cup on the balance and all tools removed, tare again. Add coarse powder gradually to **4.27 g** using its dry scoop at low height in the selected powder-control setup. Record the actual added mass. Do not shake or dump the pail. Close it promptly.
3. **Round 1, fine:** without mixing yet, tare again with the cup alone on the pan. Add **1.83 g** fine powder using its separate dry scoop; record the actual mass and close the pail. If any material spills outside the cup, stop and record the loss—do not count it as an addition to the paste or sweep it back in. Powder on the pan or cup exterior may still be counted by the balance; flag the addition/batch if the mass actually entering the cup cannot be determined.
4. Remove the cup from the balance to the tray. **Mix for 60 seconds:** gently fold the powder into the oil, scrape the bottom and walls, and gather the paste back to the center. Do not whisk, grind forcefully, or mix on the balance. Start the timer when mixing begins. Record interruptions and visible dry clumps. Scrape paste off the mixing blade into the cup as consistently as practical, then park the blade on its own rest.
5. **Round 2:** return the cup without a tool to the balance, let it stabilize and repeat steps 2–4: fresh tare, **4.27 g coarse**; fresh tare, **1.83 g fine**; then **60 seconds mixing** off the balance.
6. **Round 3:** repeat the same two weighed additions and **60 seconds mixing**.
7. After round 3, mix for **another 3 minutes**, using the same folding/scraping action. Total planned active mixing is **6 minutes**. Scrape the blade back into the cup, cover it and **rest 2 minutes**. This rest is an observation interval, not proven degassing.
8. Briefly uncover and photograph the batch with its ID. Observe whether it levels, holds a peak, is stiff, or still contains unwetted powder. Record oil separation, dry pockets, visible bubbles and difficulty scraping. Close the cup. Record the end time and any paste left on the tool or lost during handling.

Fresh taring before every addition avoids treating paste removed on the spatula as missing powder. It does **not** restore material lost during mixing. Sum the recorded ingredient additions for input mass. To measure recovered paste separately **before aliquoting or home measurements**, remove everything from the pan and clear the previous tare as the manual instructs; then weigh the **uncovered mixing cup with paste, no tool**, and subtract that cup's original empty mass without lid. Keep the exterior clean and record any cleanup loss. Never add extra oil or powder merely to restore an apparent total after mixing.

**If an addition overshoots:** write the actual mass; do not remove a guessed portion from a mixed cup, return material to stock, or hide the discrepancy. Establish a practical addition tolerance during practice and record it before A/B. A proposed starting target is within **0.02 g of each planned addition** if the balance passes the checks; this is an operator target, not a validated scientific cutoff. An out-of-target batch remains a recorded run. Any rescue by extra oil, changed mixing time or other treatment is explicitly a changed process.

**Make it**

- Respirator, glasses and gloves on. Note room temperature and humidity.
- First batch of every session: measure the reference paste's density and spread (steps below) before any batch of your own.
- Tare a labelled cup. Weigh in the oil. Write down the actual mass, not the target.

**Measure it**

- **Mixing class.** Pick one: `flows` (levels on its own), `paste` (holds a peak, spreads easily), `stiff` (hard to spread), `will_not_wet` (dry clumps remain). The last is a result: log it and stop there.

Use three fresh portions from the batch; retain the raw fill masses and do not return tested portions to the archived sample. Density is mass divided by measured cup volume. ## 6. The home thermal rig (in parallel, judged a week later)

- Cut two 40 mm blocks from the bar. Each needs two 1.6 mm holes drilled to the centre line, 5 mm and 25 mm from the test face. The upper block also needs a 6 mm hole for the heater. Ask whoever drills them to face both ends flat and square.
- Lap each test face on wet-and-dry paper taped to the float glass, working up to the finest grit.
- Compare all thermocouples in one stirred mug of water, at room temperature and near 60 °C. Record each one's offset. **Done when:** they agree within 0.1 °C after correction.
- Seat the thermocouples in their holes with a trace of reference paste. Fold each stripped wire end double before clamping it in the board's terminal.
- Stack on the ceramic tile: cooler, lower block, three 2 × 2 mm shim tabs near the corners, paste, upper block with heater, insulation round the sides, a fixed weight on top. Measure each shim stack with the micrometer.
- Wire the heater through a thermal cut-out to the bench supply. Set the current limit. **Have the wiring checked by a person before first power-on.**
- First run on the reference paste at a 200 micrometre gap. Start near 10 W and raise it only while the hottest thermocouple stays below 80 °C. Stay with it the whole time.
- When the readings stop changing, compute the result:

```sh
python3 outputs/thermal-paste-first-cycle/thermal.py mount --hot 52.0 49.6 --cold 37.4 35.1
```

- Ten separate mounts of the reference paste. **Done when:** the scatter between mounts is computed. If it is above 15% by Friday 16 October, ask about renting the needle-probe instrument instead of slipping the schedule.

At about 10 W the temperature difference along each bar is only about 2 °C, so thermocouple offsets matter. If the two bars' heat flows differ by more than about 15%, check insulation and thermocouple seating before trusting the number.
