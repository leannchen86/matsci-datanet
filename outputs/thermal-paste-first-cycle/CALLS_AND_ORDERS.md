### Questions to get answered

1. **Scope:** Can the quote include at least three measured paste thicknesses per specimen? How many fresh loadings or repeats do you recommend? Please price an additional extreme formulation separately. Three specimens at three thicknesses means at least nine thickness points, not three readings.
2. **Conditions:** What thickness range, specimen area, surface preparation, temperature and load/gap-control method suit these powders? Ask to keep the hot face at or below 80 °C. Have the engineer define a comparable protocol; fixed thickness and fixed pressure may not be independently controllable for grease.
3. **Quantity:** How many grams per specimen cover loading losses, repeats and any reserve? Which containers and storage conditions do you require? Do you need fresh paste, a specified maximum sample age, or mixing immediately before testing?
4. **Deliverables:** Request numerical thickness, impedance, temperature and load data, the conductivity fit and contact intercept, repeat results and uncertainty information. Ask separately which raw time-series/instrument files can be supplied; an Excel summary is not necessarily all raw data.
5. **Dates and price:** Ask for an itemized quote and a committed result date. The first-package spending target is about **$1,000**, not a known market price. If the full scope costs more, request both the full quote and a scientifically useful smaller scope before deciding. Do not assume four pastes fit into one day: LongWin lists about 2–4 hours per material. [Method and service](https://longwinusa.com/services/thermal-interface-material-test/)
6. **Administration:** What billing details, payment methods and deposit/cancellation terms apply? Provide personal billing or affiliation details if requested. Can you publish the supplied measurements and name the lab? Can you observe the engineer if access permits?
7. **Acceptance:** Which SDSs and composition details do they need? Is DOWSIL 340 acceptable? Arrange any visit or drop-off; published opening hours do not confirm walk-in acceptance.

Close with: “Please email the scope, sample requirements, price and receipt/result dates. May I have your name and direct contact for the technical follow-up?”

If no local date is confirmed by Wednesday 7 October, pursue the mail-in booking. Ship only after the receiving lab accepts the job and the samples are ready; do not dispatch unfinished or unaccepted material on October 7.

## 4 Arrange handling and the later thermal rig

Before opening powder, send the actual SDSs, room description/photos and intended batch quantities to an industrial hygienist for a short paid review of dust control, PPE and cleanup. [AIHA's consultant directory](https://info.aiha.org/consultants-listing/) is a starting point. Ask: “Do you accept a self-paying individual for a small home materials experiment, what information do you need, and what is the fee and earliest review date?”

Ask the reviewer which respirator and fit-testing route, if any, suit the work. Purchase selected PPE through an established supplier such as [Enviro Safety](https://www.envirosafetyproducts.com/) or [Grainger](https://www.grainger.com/); do not automatically order a medium facepiece. Glasses and gloves remain part of the handling kit. A particulate filter is not a vapor filter, and owning a respirator does not establish that the room is suitable.

Ask the local waste service how it accepts material from this independent research activity. Identify the zinc-oxide commercial paste and contaminated wipes separately. Store waste as instructed; do not assume household eligibility. Exact service depends on your address.

Keep the home thermal rig as parallel design work. Before ordering its electronics, have the proposed wiring, thermocouple electrical isolation, connectors, cooler mounting/load path and thermal cutoff checked. Ask a machine shop for a quote to drill and face the two bars once the drawing is settled. Do not buy a drill press solely on the assumption that drilling will also produce flat, square measurement faces.

The existing README contains candidate rig parts, not a verified complete assembly. For this first cart, defer the heater/logger/cooler bundle, drill press, boron nitride, extra oil viscosities, vacuum mixer and copper chemicals. Revisit the rig promptly after its design review; the independent laboratory quote proceeds in parallel.

## 5 Fix sample allocation before mixing gate batches

Use this mass budget for every laboratory-bound batch:

`batch mass >= lab requirement + 3 × measured cup volume × estimated density + 2 × spread dose + retained archive + handling losses`

For illustration, three 1.25 mL fills at 2.62 g/mL consume 9.83 g. Two 1 g spreads bring that to 11.83 g, leaving 13.17 g from a 25 g batch before losses and any archive. A lab requiring 30 g cannot be served from that batch. Increase and rehearse the batch size before freezing the mixing protocol, or agree a smaller submission with the lab. Do not silently return tested paste to the retained sample to make the arithmetic work.

Two independent controls test preparation repeatability. A pooled transfer lot for two labs is a separate experiment and receives its own ID, extra mixing history and mass budget. Obtain both labs' required masses first, reserve QA material separately, then split into accepted containers. Record sample age, storage and remix instructions. Blind predicted outcomes and the other lab's result, while disclosing material identity and SDSs needed for acceptance.

DOWSIL 340 is a commercial comparator. Ask the lab to explain its calibration/QC and measurement uncertainty; do not use agreement with the datasheet's typical 0.67 W/m·K as a calibration certificate.
