## Two decisions

**Which alumina is the record lot.** The McMaster powders arrive a day or two sooner and each comes with a lot certificate, but they are not the planned white fused grades. Particle shape and size set the mixing limit, trapped air and spread, so the two sources are not interchangeable.

- Option A: make McMaster the record lot for the whole campaign. Nothing to wait for. Ask McMaster first whether each powder is fused or calcined and for a size distribution. Buy the whole campaign's quantity in one order: the coarse part lists two countries of origin.
- Option B: keep the planned grades as the record lot and treat McMaster as practice material. Then repeat a short shakedown when the planned lots arrive.

Either way, never mix sources inside the gate series, and decide before the protocol is frozen.

**Whether to open powder before a qualified person has answered.** No consultant with a quick, published small-consultation offer was found. If powder stays sealed until then, Tuesday is no-powder work: balance checks, calibrating the density measure with water, and density and spread of the reference paste.

Keep practice runs in their own campaign in the logging tool (for example `paste-practice`), not just under a different `_lot`. `verify` counts exact repeats from the recipe settings and ignores the lot.

## Machining the rig blocks

This is the one thing driving does not speed up much. A local machine shop's own site says about two weeks for prototype work. Fastest sensible route: buy the bar sawn to length on Tuesday, phone two or three shops with a one-page sketch and ask for the earliest day, and if nobody offers under a week, drill at home and lap by hand. Blocks made that way are recorded as "saw-cut, hand-lapped, flatness not measured" and are for shakedown only.
