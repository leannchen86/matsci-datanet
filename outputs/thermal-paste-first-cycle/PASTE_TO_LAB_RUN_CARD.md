# Thermal paste recipe and laboratory comparison

Prepared 9 October 2026. Method **PASTE-P2-v1**, prospective and not yet executed. Follow [CHECKLIST.md](CHECKLIST.md) for completion status. This card supersedes the B001 preparation instructions for the next paste batches.

Make two independent batches from the ingredients already owned, then compare their resistance to heat flow with purchased DOWSIL 340. This is an exploratory thermal-interface-material application test. It does not qualify a paste for a working chip, establish dielectric safety or lifetime, or prove that experimental histories improve model training.

The next available IDs are tentatively **B002 and B003**; check the registry before assigning them. B001 keeps its original identity and record. Its photographs, feel and 0.067 g input-minus-jar discrepancy do not establish the composition or thermal effect of residue.

## What changes from B001

Keep the same intended mass recipe. Improve the weighing record, incorporation of material from the walls, timing and endpoint observation. Use the same vessel type, spatula and material lots for both new batches. A smooth, wide-opening approximately 60 mL cup with a lid is practical if available; the existing dedicated glass-jar type can be used if a dry rehearsal shows the spatula reaches the bottom and walls comfortably. Record the chosen vessel dimensions and do not switch halfway through the pair.

Weigh **the cup alone for every addition**. Park the mixing spatula on one dedicated, rigid, clean rest, preweighed together with the spatula. The rest can be a small clean dish or another suitable container; it must fit fully on the balance with the tool, without external contact. Keep both throughout the run. Do not wipe paste away between rounds or smear it onto the upper rim. Material still on the tool can re-enter during the next mixing stage; any final residue is measured separately. Total residue mass cannot tell us how much oil versus powder it contains, so it cannot justify a recipe correction.

**The first new batch can also be a lab specimen.** We no longer require a disposable extra practice batch before this exploratory comparison. Before mixing, record whether the batch is intended for the comparison, and use these process criteria:

- The balance and handling checks below are complete, the selected arrangement passes dry rehearsal, and targets, observations and timing are written down beforehand.
- Actual additions are recorded, with a working target of within 0.020 g of each intended addition. This is a preparation target, not a validated material-performance tolerance. An addition outside that target is a declared formulation deviation, **still eligible for exploratory lab testing when the actual masses are known**. Do not replace it merely for missing the target or because it later performs poorly. Analyze its actual recipe and do not attribute its difference from the other specimen solely to mixing or test repeatability.
- There are no unaccounted spills, unknown ingredient additions, unrecorded corrections or unresolved contamination. Record actual mixing/rest times and interruptions; an altered mixing schedule is a method deviation.
- **Appearance, stiffness and thermal performance are not eligibility filters.** Keep poor results. If the lab cannot load the paste, retain that as a measured handling outcome rather than substituting a better-looking specimen without disclosure.

If the first run requires a method change, keep it as an exploratory specimen with the deviation. Write the revised method before a new run; two specimens prepared under different methods are not an exact repeat pair. Do not retrospectively rename B001 or erase any run. Even two well-recorded batches provide only preliminary information about preparation variability.

## Execute one batch

1. **Identify and weigh the empty equipment.** Label the clean, dry cup with its unused batch ID before weighing. Record date, operator, material lots, balance configuration and room temperature if measured. Clear any previous tare. Record the empty labeled cup **without lid or tool**, then the clean, dry **spatula and its rest together**. Both weighings are gross readings with no old tare. Keep these two baselines.

2. **Add oil.** Put only the cup on the balance, tare to zero and add **6.710 g oil**. Let the reading settle without touching the cup; record the actual addition and time. Take no material back out to chase the last decimal.

3. **Round 1.** With the cup alone on the pan, tare and add **4.270 g coarse alumina**, using its dedicated dry scoop in the selected dust-control arrangement. Record the stable net reading and close the stock. Tare again and add **1.830 g fine alumina** with its other scoop; record and close. Remove the cup to the tray. Mix for **60 seconds of active mixing**, gently folding and pressing powder into the oil, scraping the base and walls toward the main paste. Use the final approximately 10 seconds to gather wall coatings down into the bulk. Do not whisk, grind, or mix on the balance. Park the spatula on its dedicated rest without wiping it clean.

4. **Round 2.** Repeat exactly: fresh cup-only tare, **4.270 g coarse**; fresh tare, **1.830 g fine**; then **60 seconds** mixing off the balance, returning the same spatula to the paste. Record actuals and park it on its rest afterward. Do not subtract residue from a powder target.

5. **Round 3 and final mix.** Repeat the same two additions and **60 seconds** mixing. Immediately continue for **another 180 seconds** with the same folding/scraping method. Planned active mixing totals **360 seconds**. Record actual active times and pauses separately. If dry clumps remain, record them; do not silently extend mixing, add oil, heat or otherwise rescue the batch. If a spill, airborne dust or another handling problem occurs, stop the affected operation and deal with it under the selected handling procedure; the event stays in the record.

6. **Rest and observe.** Gather wall paste toward the bulk during the timed final mixing, park the tool on its rest, cover the cup and start a **120-second rest timer**. This is an observation interval, not a cure or proven degassing step. At its end, uncover, photograph the surface, then use the same spatula to draw one channel through the central paste down to the base and lift it once. Record a fixed-view 60-second video starting at the lift. Note whether the channel is still open at **10 and 60 seconds**, whether a peak persists, visible dry pockets/bubbles/oil, and how it feels to move the spatula. Use the same action and camera view for both batches. These are descriptive observations, not viscosity or homogeneity measurements. Park the tool on its rest again.

7. **Account for the material before any sampling or cleanup.** With the pan empty, clear the last tare. Weigh the uncovered cup plus paste, without the tool. Separately weigh the current spatula plus its original rest, including paste on either. Let both readings stabilize; nothing may touch another surface. Do not include the cup lid in one weighing but not its baseline. Keep cup exteriors and the pan clean during preparation. Any residue still attached to the cup exterior is already included in cup gross mass, although it is not usable paste; note it without counting it again as a removal. Residue on an unweighed lid, a wipe or another object lies outside the two weighed assemblies. Record such removals and whether their mass is known. Powder spilled onto the pan during an addition can corrupt the apparent ingredient mass and must be flagged.

   ```text
   jar-associated paste = (final cup + paste) − (initial empty labeled cup)
   tool/rest residue = (final tool + rest + residue) − (initial clean tool + rest)
   unaccounted difference = sum(actual additions) − jar-associated paste
                           − tool/rest residue − separately measured removals
   ```

   This is an accounting check, not a component analysis. The removals term covers only material no longer on either weighed assembly; do not double-count attached residue. Report unmeasured removals and measurement limitations beside the difference. Count the current residue once; never add successive residue readings together as though they were distinct losses. Paste coating the jar wall is already included in jar-associated mass, although it may differ from the material sampled from the bulk. **Do not add oil or powder to force mass closure.**

8. **Close and preserve.** Cap the jar, add contents/method/date/time to the record and keep it upright in labeled secondary containment at stable indoor room temperature, away from food, sunlight and heaters. Record actual storage conditions and duration. Do not refrigerate, heat, dilute or remix without a recorded method decision. Photograph the completed sheet and retain original images. Clean the tool only after the final accounting, using the selected SDS-based cleanup method; do not rinse experimental residues into drains. The next batch starts with completely clean, dry equipment.

Record targets and actuals separately. An overshoot stays recorded; do not remove a guessed amount from a mixed cup or return material to stock. Use the printed card without waiting for chat between routine additions. Ask for help for a genuine ambiguity or unexpected event, and record the interruption.

## Prepare the second batch and reference

Prepare a second **fresh, independently weighed and mixed** batch with the same selected method, ingredient lots, vessel geometry and observation routine. Prefer the next session/day if that fits the lab's age instructions; record actual dates and the resulting different ages. Splitting one jar into two containers is not independent preparation. If method changes are necessary, version them first and report which specimens share a method.

Leave DOWSIL 340 as purchased: no powder, oil or curing treatment. Photograph its product/lot information and transfer only with the reference tool if LongWin requests a separate container. Dow describes it as a non-flowing compound for thermal coupling of electrical devices and PCB assemblies to heat sinks; stiffness alone therefore does not determine application failure. Its published conductivity is not a calibration certificate. [Dow product information](https://www.dow.com/en-us/pdp.dowsil-340-heat-sink-compound.01015443z.html).

Package in accepted containers under the agreed conditioning instructions. A **5 g allocation per specimen** is our proposed surplus over the lab's approximately 1 mL request, not a requirement or a measured volume conversion. Confirm it with the lab. Weigh each empty destination with its lid, then sealed with paste; net amount is their difference. Label parent batch, specimen ID, contents, preparation/packing time and net mass; retain the rest as a separately identified archive. If mixing vessels are accepted, avoid unnecessary transfer and still supply the correct mass definition. Record remixing and transfer timing rather than discarding any separated oil.

## Define the lab result before submitting

Ask LongWin to quote **two independently prepared homemade pastes plus DOWSIL 340**, with these requirements agreed before testing:

1. Primary comparison: **area-normalized thermal impedance** of the complete paste interface under comparable measured thickness, contact pressure and temperature, with common test surfaces and loading/conditioning procedure. Lower impedance under those matched conditions is the useful direction. It is not necessarily a measured improvement over bare contact unless a separate bare-contact control is actually run.
2. Request three engineer-selected thicknesses per material if feasible, suitable for the actual particles. Clarify gap control versus pressure control: both may not be independently imposed. Record actual thickness, force/pressure, mean and boundary temperatures, area, load history and dwell/stability conditions for each point. Do not treat unmatched points as a direct composition comparison or force an unsupported extrapolation to thinner gaps.
3. Request reload/repeat measurements at one common comparison condition for each material, priced separately if needed. Ask for sample-specific uncertainty and the lab's repeatability information. Without reloads, between-batch differences also contain unknown loading/testing variability.
4. Obtain numerical Excel/CSV data and the report, including per-point thermal impedance, thickness uncertainty, repeats, and the fitted apparent conductivity/contact term **only where the fit is appropriate**. A thickness series is not three independently prepared batches. Preserve lab originals unchanged.

LongWin publicly offers ASTM D5470-based grease testing, side-by-side comparisons and Excel output; the exact scope still requires acceptance. [LongWin service description](https://longwinusa.com/services/thermal-interface-material-test/).

Before seeing results, specify that we will report every submitted sample and compare the two homemade specimens with each other and with the measured reference, taking test uncertainty and actual conditions into account. A failed loading, high impedance or disagreement between batches informs the next preparation change. Agreement between two samples is encouraging but cannot establish general reproducibility. Good thermal performance would justify an application-specific follow-up involving aging/thermal cycling, material compatibility and relevant electrical requirements before use on a valuable device.

The deliverable is **two batch histories, traceable specimens and measured reference comparisons**. It is not just two jars, and it is not yet a model-training-value experiment. Once the thermal records exist, a separate prospective test can ask whether richer histories improve a prediction or experimental decision beyond recipe labels alone.
