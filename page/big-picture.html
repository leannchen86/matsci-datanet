ML for materials discovery · everything so far, on one page · updated 19 Sep 2026

Open lab data has no trusted labels and no fair exam.

That is the whole story of 15 chats and 10 reports. ImageNet changed vision because it paired labels people trusted with a hidden-label test with fixed rules. Measured (not simulated) materials data has neither yet, for four reasons. All four can be worked on from a laptop.

An “ImageNet moment”
ML progress on real lab data becomes measurable
=
Trusted labels
records you can train on without inheriting their mistakes
+
A fair exam
a test set and a marking rule that reward more than memorising

Why the labels are not trusted

1
Records contain mistakes that nothing catches
Label noise with no validator. Unit slips, sign flips, simulated files filed as measured.
2
Records do not say how the sample was made or measured
Missing features. Two samples with the same formula are different “images” of one class.

Why the exam is not fair

3
Test questions leak into training
Random splits test on formulas and papers the model has already seen, like splitting by scan instead of by patient.
4
Some tasks have no answer key, and no written marking rule
Ground truth is one annotator’s opinion, and the grader’s string matching decides the score.

Volume is not the missing piece. Simulated databases hold over a million materials. Open records that pair a recipe with its raw X-ray scan number about 1,400 (1,035 + 352).

What to build next

five planners, then adversarial checkers · both headline claims got smaller · eval check added 19 Sep
the recommendation · after adversarial checking

Start with a one-to-two-day test, not a build: is the X-ray marking problem real, or a bug in one marker?

One 40-row table answers it. (“Marking” is the scoring rule that decides whether a program’s answer counts as correct: the metric.) The answer decides whether the 2–3 week reusable marker is worth building, or whether a fix and a test file inside your own project is enough. Both outcomes are useful and both are cheap. Whether other people need such a marker is a separate question, answered by outside evidence, not by this table.

  • It is the smallest step that decides the most. The famous swing from 12 to 32 of 40 turned out to have three causes, and only one is a marking rule. Nobody has counted what is left once formula spelling is cleaned up (the same compound written as a different string, such as “Ni1.875O2” for NiO).
  • It has a sure first user: you. A pilot probe beside your project scores 0.309 under strict marking and 0.745 under lenient marking (n = 55).
  • No download, no model, no lab, no message to anyone. The 40 scans and stored results already sit in your own project folders. It needs two yeses from you: a folder name for the rescue copy, and read-only access to those folders.
Eval check · 19 Sep · is this evaluation work, and is it set up right?

Yes, mostly. About two thirds of the items check an exam: its metric, answer key, split or error bars. ML calls that benchmark auditing; measurement labs call it method validation. The rest is data-error reporting and file housekeeping, which one skeptic counts too (as label-noise and contamination checks). It fits the “fair exam” half of the picture in section 01. It does not cover the collecting half, and month one produces fixes for your own project, not something a stranger can load. Weigh that at the week-2 sitting. Only the partner-lab item builds a new exam.

Four reviewers (ML benchmark science, measurement science, materials-ML benchmarks, test validity) scored the plan against 56 published principles. Four skeptics then attacked the reviews. All eight say: go ahead with the 40-row test. The skeptics put the writing needed first at about one hour (one reviewer had asked for 2–3). The tally: 6 met, 45 partly met, 1 not yet relevant, 4 rated broken. Of those 4 the skeptics downgraded two, threw out one (it rested on a misread paper) and upheld one: calling the weighed answer key “certain”. With nearly every row “partly”, the tally says little. The changes are the useful part, and they are already folded into the cards below.

Weighed answers are certainThey are known to within ingredient purity, weighing error and any reaction during mixing. Weighed mixtures are a floor test: failing is informative, passing is not proof.
A hidden weighed exam is the prizeThe scarce thing is fresh, verified powders every round, plus a key holder who stays out of the ranking. That is lab work; from a desk it may be out of reach. Software has already been scored on contest mixtures after the fact (2019, 2021), so the novelty shrinks to a recurring hidden-recipe exam for phase-naming software on the kinds of inorganic compounds synthesis labs make (the one standing contest is on clay minerals).

Two samples with the same formula can measure very differently: about 6% spread between labs on one specimen, against a 32.7% median gap across papers (different statistics, shown only for scale).
