ML for materials discovery · everything so far, on one page · 18 Sep 2026

Open lab data has no trusted labels and no fair exam.

That is the whole story of 15 chats and 10 reports. ImageNet changed vision because it paired labels people trusted with a test nobody could game. Measured (not simulated) materials data has neither yet, for four reasons. All four can be worked on from a laptop.

An “ImageNet moment”
ML progress on real lab data becomes measurable
=
Trusted labels
records you can train on without inheriting their mistakes
+
A fair exam
a test set and a marking rule that cannot be passed by memorising

Why the labels are not trusted

1
Records contain mistakes that nothing catches
Label noise with no validator. Unit slips, sign flips, simulated files filed as measured.
2
Records do not say how the sample was made or measured
Missing features. Two samples with the same formula are different “images” of one class.

Why the exam is not fair

3
Test questions leak into training
Random splits put the same paper on both sides, like splitting by scan instead of by patient.
4
Some tasks have no answer key, and no written marking rule
Ground truth is one annotator’s opinion, and the grader’s string matching decides the score.

Volume is not the missing piece. Simulated databases hold over a million materials. Open records that pair a recipe with its raw X-ray scan number about 1,400 (1,035 + 352).

04

What to build next

five independent planners agree on the first step · stress-test still running
the recommendation

Make the exam fair before making anything bigger. Start with the X-ray marking kit, inside your own project.

Both earlier recommendations are the same kind of work: making the exam fair. They differ only in where to commit a month or more. Five independent planners, each given everything on this page and a different lens (quick wins, reuse by others, fit to you, contrarian, connect-the-threads), all landed on the same first step.

  • A first user already exists: you. Your dara-conform project needs a fair marker, and its own written fallback is “shrink to the evaluation harness”. The thermoelectric tools have no named user yet.
  • In X-ray data the input really contains the answer. In thermoelectrics a formula-only model has a 15.0% error floor even with perfect labels, so a benchmark on today’s data mostly measures missing information.
  • The marking rule moves the score from 12 to 32 of 40. That sits upstream of every X-ray exam anyone will ever run, and fixing it is desk work.
The threadHow much the exam design moves the scoreHow far apart the contestants are

The one sentence that ties every thread together: how the exam is set and marked moves the score more than the contestants differ from each other. No single report says it, because each saw only its own half.

Twelve words are enough

WordWhat it isThink of it as
Composition vs specimenThe chemical formula, versus one physical piece of material with its own making history.Class name vs individual image
PhaseOne crystalline compound inside a powder. A powder is usually a mix of several.One label in a multi-label problem

ThermoelectricA material that turns a temperature difference into electricity.The application domain
